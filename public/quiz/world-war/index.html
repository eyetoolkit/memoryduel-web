World War 2 Quiz

World War II is the most heavily documented event in modern history, which makes it easy to recognise and surprisingly hard to date precisely. Most people know 1945 and D-Day; far fewer can place Midway, Stalingrad or the Potsdam Conference on a timeline without stopping to think.

This quiz has 22 questions drawn from both theatres — European and Pacific — with an emphasis on the moments that changed the direction of the war rather than on troop counts nobody can remember.

Every answer carries a one-line explanation with the year attached, so the answer key doubles as a compressed timeline. It sits directly below the quiz.

Answer key

All 22 questions, with the correct answer and the reasoning.

  1. In which year did the Second World War begin in Europe?

    Answer: 1939

    Germany invaded Poland on 1 September 1939, and Britain and France declared war two days later.

  2. The German invasion of which country triggered Britain's declaration of war?

    Answer: Poland

    Britain and France had guaranteed Polish independence, so the invasion on 1 September 1939 forced their hand.

  3. In which year did Japan attack Pearl Harbor?

    Answer: 1941

    The attack came on 7 December 1941 and brought the United States into the war the following day.

  4. On which date did the D-Day landings take place?

    Answer: 6 June 1944

    The Normandy landings on 6 June 1944 were the largest seaborne invasion in history and opened the Western Front.

  5. Who was the leader of Nazi Germany during the war?

    Answer: Adolf Hitler

    Hitler was chancellor from 1933 and took the title of Führer in 1934, holding both offices until his suicide in 1945.

  6. Who was British prime minister for most of the war?

    Answer: Winston Churchill

    Churchill became prime minister in May 1940 and led the country until July 1945, when Attlee replaced him before the war in the Pacific ended.

  7. Which battle is usually called the turning point of the Eastern Front?

    Answer: Stalingrad

    The German Sixth Army surrendered at Stalingrad in February 1943, after being encircled through the winter.

  8. Which two Japanese cities were struck by atomic bombs?

    Answer: Hiroshima and Nagasaki

    Hiroshima was bombed on 6 August 1945 and Nagasaki on 9 August 1945, six days before Japan announced its surrender.

  9. In which year did the Second World War end?

    Answer: 1945

    Germany surrendered in May 1945 and Japan in September 1945, ending the war in both theatres.

  10. What was the code name for the Allied invasion of Normandy?

    Answer: Operation Overlord

    Overlord was the invasion plan itself; the naval and airborne assault phase on 6 June was code-named Neptune.

  11. What was the German air force called?

    Answer: Luftwaffe

    The Luftwaffe was the air branch, while the Wehrmacht was the armed forces as a whole and the Kriegsmarine the navy.

  12. Who was US president for most of the Second World War?

    Answer: Franklin D. Roosevelt

    Roosevelt led the country from 1933 until his death in April 1945, less than four weeks before Germany surrendered. Truman then took over.

  13. Which three countries formed the core of the Axis alliance?

    Answer: Germany, Italy, Japan

    The Tripartite Pact of September 1940 formalised the alliance between Germany, Italy and Japan.

  14. In which year was the Battle of Britain fought?

    Answer: 1940

    The air campaign ran from July to October 1940, and its failure persuaded Germany to abandon plans to invade Britain.

  15. What was the name of the Nazi secret police?

    Answer: Gestapo

    The Gestapo was the secret state police, formed in 1933 and run from 1936 by Heinrich Himmler.

  16. Which 1945 conference was held after Germany's surrender to settle the terms of its occupation?

    Answer: Potsdam

    Potsdam, held in July and August 1945, agreed the principles for occupied Germany — denazification, demilitarisation and decentralisation. The four zones themselves had been drawn up earlier by the European Advisory Commission and confirmed at Yalta.

  17. Which 1942 battle is considered the turning point in the Pacific?

    Answer: Midway

    At Midway in June 1942 the Japanese lost four fleet carriers, which ended their ability to take the offensive in the Pacific.

  18. What was the systematic murder of European Jews by Nazi Germany called?

    Answer: The Holocaust

    The Holocaust killed roughly six million Jews, along with millions of Roma, disabled people and others the regime classified as undesirable.

  19. Which city was besieged for nearly 900 days during the war?

    Answer: Leningrad

    The siege of Leningrad lasted from September 1941 to January 1944, and the civilian death toll was in the hundreds of thousands.

  20. In which year did Italy surrender to the Allies?

    Answer: 1943

    Italy announced an armistice in September 1943, after the Allied landings in Sicily. Germany then occupied northern Italy and fought on.

  21. Which country did Germany invade in June 1941, opening the Eastern Front?

    Answer: The Soviet Union

    Operation Barbarossa began on 22 June 1941 and eventually involved more troops than any other theatre of the war.

  22. What was the Allied programme to build an atomic bomb called?

    Answer: The Manhattan Project

    The Manhattan Project ran from 1942 and produced the weapons used at Hiroshima and Nagasaki. Trinity was the name of the first test.

What this quiz covers

The questions are split roughly two to one between the European and Pacific theatres, which matches how the war is taught in most English-language schools — but the Pacific questions are not an afterthought, because Midway and the island campaign decide several of the harder items.

Nothing here depends on memorising casualty figures. Where a number matters, it is a date, and the explanation gives the context that makes the date stick.

  • The timeline: 1939 in Poland, 1940 over Britain, 1941 at Pearl Harbor, 1944 in Normandy, 1945 at Potsdam.
  • Turning points: Stalingrad on the Eastern Front, Midway in the Pacific, and why historians single those two out.
  • The leaders: Hitler, Churchill, Roosevelt, Stalin, and which of them changed sides during the war.
  • Code names and organisations: Operation Overlord, the Luftwaffe, the Gestapo, the Manhattan Project.
  • The end of the war: the two atomic bombings, the surrender of Germany and Japan, and the occupation of Germany.

Frequently asked questions

When did World War II start and end?
In Europe it began on 1 September 1939 with the German invasion of Poland and ended on 8 May 1945 with Germany's surrender. In the Pacific it ended on 2 September 1945, after the atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki and Japan's surrender.
When did the United States enter the war?
On 8 December 1941, the day after Japan attacked Pearl Harbor. The United States declared war on Japan that day, and Germany declared war on the United States four days later.
Which countries were the Axis powers?
Germany, Italy and Japan were the three principal Axis powers. Hungary, Romania, Bulgaria and several others joined or were absorbed into the Axis at various points.
Why was Stalingrad a turning point?
The encirclement and surrender of the German Sixth Army in early 1943 ended any realistic chance of Germany defeating the Soviet Union, and from then on the Eastern Front moved westwards almost continuously.
How is this quiz different from a World War I quiz?
It covers only 1939 to 1945. Questions about the Treaty of Versailles, the Central Powers or the 1914 assassination in Sarajevo belong to the First World War and are deliberately excluded here.
