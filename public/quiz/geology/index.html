Answer key

All fifteen questions above, with the correct answer and the reasoning.

  1. What is the name of the outermost layer of the Earth where life exists?

    Answer: The Crust

    The crust is the thin outermost layer of the Earth where all known life exists.

  2. What is the hardest natural substance found in nature?

    Answer: Diamond

    Diamond ranks 10 on the Mohs hardness scale, the highest of any natural material.

  3. What is the term for the slow movement of glaciers or ice sheets under their own weight?

    Answer: Glaciation

    Glaciation refers to the process where glaciers and ice sheets move slowly, eroding the landscape and depositing debris.

  4. Which of these is NOT a type of rock?

    Answer: Mineral

    Minerals are the building blocks of rocks, not rocks themselves. The three main rock types are igneous, sedimentary, and metamorphic.

  5. What is the phenomenon known as the 'Great Oxygenation Event'?

    Answer: The release of oxygen by early photosynthetic bacteria

    The Great Oxygenation Event occurred when cyanobacteria began producing large amounts of oxygen, transforming Earth's atmosphere.

  6. What is the hardest naturally occurring substance on Earth?

    Answer: Diamond

    Diamond is the hardest natural material known, scoring a 10 on the Mohs hardness scale.

  7. What is the phenomenon where the Earth's poles switch their magnetic orientation called?

    Answer: Magnetic Reversal

    Geomagnetic reversals are changes in the Earth's magnetic field where the North and South magnetic poles swap places.

  8. What is the primary gas found in Earth's atmosphere?

    Answer: Nitrogen

    Nitrogen makes up about 78% of Earth's atmosphere, while oxygen makes up about 21%.

  9. Which is the highest mountain in the solar system?

    Answer: Olympus Mons

    Olympus Mons on Mars rises about 21 km, the tallest known mountain.

  10. Which of these is the most abundant element in the Earth's crust?

    Answer: Oxygen

    Oxygen is the most abundant element in the Earth's crust, making up about 46% of its mass, mostly in the form of oxides.

  11. The 'Ring of Fire' is a major zone of tectonic activity that surrounds which ocean?

    Answer: Pacific Ocean

    The Pacific Ring of Fire is a horseshoe-shaped belt around the Pacific Ocean where a large number of earthquakes and volcanic eruptions occur.

  12. What is the approximate age of the Earth?

    Answer: 4.5 billion years

    Radiometric dating and other evidence suggest Earth is approximately 4.54 billion years old, which is about one-third the age of the universe.

  13. Which layer of Earth's interior is liquid and composed mainly of iron and nickel?

    Answer: Outer core

    The outer core is liquid and its convective motion generates Earth's magnetic field.

  14. What is the name of the supercontinent that existed roughly 335 million years ago?

    Answer: Pangaea

    Pangaea was the supercontinent that assembled during the late Paleozoic and early Mesozoic eras. Laurasia and Gondwana were the two major landmasses it later split into, and Rodinia was an earlier supercontinent.

  15. Which biogeochemical cycle is most tightly linked to the long-term weathering of silicate rocks?

    Answer: Carbon cycle

    Silicate weathering draws down atmospheric CO₂ over geological timescales, regulating climate.
