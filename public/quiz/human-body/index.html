Human Body Quiz

Human anatomy is taught badly more often than it is taught well: the parts get memorised and the numbers get muddled. How many bones does an adult have? Most people say 206 and then hesitate when asked how many a newborn has.

This quiz has 22 questions on the body as a working system — organs, bones, blood, and the small set of numbers that are worth actually knowing. Every answer is explained, so a wrong guess is corrected with a reason rather than a red cross.

Answer key

All 22 questions, with the correct answer and the reasoning.

  1. What is the largest organ in the human body?

    Answer: Skin

    The skin is the body's largest organ, covering the entire external surface.

  2. Which human organ can regenerate to a significant extent after removal of a portion?

    Answer: Liver

    The liver can regrow to its original size within weeks after up to 70% of it is surgically removed.

  3. Which organ in the human body produces insulin?

    Answer: Pancreas

    The pancreas secretes insulin, regulating blood glucose levels.

  4. How many bones are in the adult human body?

    Answer: 206 bones

    An adult human body has 206 bones. Babies are born with around 270 bones, but many fuse together as we grow.

  5. Which part of the human body has the highest density of sweat glands?

    Answer: Palms of the hands

    The palms and soles contain the greatest number of eccrine sweat glands per square centimetre.

  6. Which rare blood type is known as 'golden blood'?

    Answer: Rh-null

    Rh-null, known as 'golden blood', lacks all Rh antigens and is extremely rare.

  7. What is the most common blood type among humans?

    Answer: O positive

    O positive is the most common blood type among the general population.

  8. Which human organ contains the highest percentage of water?

    Answer: Lungs

    The lungs are about 83 % water, the highest water content of any major organ.

  9. Which nerve is the longest in the human body?

    Answer: Sciatic nerve

    The sciatic nerve runs from the lower spine to the foot and is the longest and thickest nerve in the body.

  10. Which organ is responsible for pumping blood in humans?

    Answer: Heart

    The heart’s muscular contractions drive blood through the circulatory system.

  11. Which human organ is responsible for filtering blood?

    Answer: Kidneys

    The kidneys remove waste products and excess substances from the blood, forming urine.

  12. What is the smallest bone in the human body?

    Answer: The stapes

    The stapes, a tiny bone in the middle ear, is the smallest bone in the human body. It is smaller than a grain of rice.

  13. How many chambers does the human heart have?

    Answer: Four

    Four: two atria on top and two ventricles below. The right side pumps to the lungs, the left side to the rest of the body.

  14. Which blood type is considered the universal donor?

    Answer: O-

    O- blood lacks A, B antigens and Rh factor, making it compatible with all blood types.

  15. Which part of the brain controls balance and coordination?

    Answer: Cerebellum

    The cerebellum sits at the back of the brain and fine-tunes movement, posture and balance.

  16. Which vitamin does the skin produce when exposed to sunlight?

    Answer: Vitamin D

    Ultraviolet light lets the skin synthesise vitamin D, which is why short daily sun exposure matters for bone health.

  17. Which organ in the human body is primarily responsible for detoxification?

    Answer: Liver

    The liver filters blood, metabolizes drugs, and breaks down toxins.

  18. What organ system is responsible for transporting blood throughout the body?

    Answer: Circulatory system

    The circulatory system, including the heart and blood vessels, transports blood.

  19. How many teeth does an adult human typically have?

    Answer: 32

    A full set of adult teeth includes 32 teeth, including the four wisdom teeth that may or may not emerge.

  20. What is the primary function of hemoglobin in the human body?

    Answer: To carry oxygen in the blood

    Hemoglobin is a protein in red blood cells that carries oxygen from the lungs to the body's tissues and returns carbon dioxide from the tissues back to the lungs.

  21. Which type of blood cell is responsible for fighting infections in the human body?

    Answer: White blood cells

    White blood cells (leukocytes) are the primary components of the immune system and help defend the body against infections and foreign invaders.

  22. What is the longest bone in the human body?

    Answer: Femur

    The femur, or thigh bone, is roughly a quarter of a person's height and is also the strongest bone in the body.

What this quiz covers

The questions favour things you can check against your own body — teeth, resting pulse, the bone that is hardest to break — because those are the facts that survive without revision. Pure nomenclature is kept to a minimum; there is no question that asks you to name a muscle in Latin.

Two of the harder items are about records rather than structure, and both are flagged in the explanation as trivia: interesting, but not something a biology exam would ask for.

  • Organs and what they do: the liver's regeneration, the kidney's filtering, the pancreas and insulin, the lungs and water content.
  • Bones: 206 in an adult, around 270 at birth, and the smallest one in the body.
  • Blood: the most common type, the universal donor, and the rare type nicknamed golden blood.
  • Nerves and senses: the longest nerve, and the parts of the body with the densest supply of sweat glands.
  • Everyday numbers: how many teeth an adult has, how many chambers the heart has, and the normal resting heart rate.

Frequently asked questions

What is the largest organ in the human body?
The skin. It covers roughly two square metres in an adult and accounts for about 15 percent of body weight, which makes it both the largest organ by area and one of the heaviest.
How many bones are in the human body?
206 in a typical adult. Babies are born with around 270, and the count falls because some bones fuse during growth — the skull and the pelvis are the clearest examples.
What is the smallest bone in the human body?
The stapes, in the middle ear. It is about three millimetres long and transmits sound vibrations from the eardrum to the inner ear.
Which organ can regenerate itself?
The liver. It can regrow a substantial part of itself after surgery or injury, which is why living-donor liver transplants are possible.
Which blood type is the universal donor?
O negative. It carries neither A nor B antigens nor the RhD antigen, so it can be given to patients of any blood type in an emergency.
