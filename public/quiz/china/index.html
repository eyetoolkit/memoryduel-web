Answer key

All fifteen questions above, with the correct answer and the reasoning.

  1. Which famous wall was built to protect China's northern borders?

    Answer: The Great Wall of China

    The Great Wall of China is a series of fortifications built over centuries to protect Chinese states against invasions from the north.

  2. What color is traditionally worn during Chinese New Year to symbolize good luck?

    Answer: Red

    Red is the lucky color in Chinese culture, worn and displayed during Lunar New Year to ward off evil and attract fortune.

  3. The Great Wall, a series of fortifications made of stone, brick, and other materials, was primarily built by which empire?

    Answer: The Han Dynasty of China

    While sections were built by various Chinese dynasties, the Han Dynasty (206 BC – 220 AD) significantly extended the wall to protect against northern invaders.

  4. The Great Wall is located in which modern country?

    Answer: China

    The Great Wall of China was built over centuries to protect Chinese states and empires against northern invasions.

  5. Which traditional Chinese philosophical system emphasizes the concept of 'Wu Wei' (effortless action or non-forcing)?

    Answer: Taoism (Daoism)

    Taoism, attributed to Laozi, teaches living in harmony with the 'Tao' (the Way) of the universe, advocating simplicity and spontaneity rather than rigid rule.

  6. What is the name of the traditional Chinese New Year dance performed with a long, colorful creature?

    Answer: Dragon Dance

    The Dragon Dance is performed during Chinese New Year and other festivals, symbolizing power, dignity, and good luck.

  7. The Chinese New Year, also known as the Spring Festival, is based on which calendar system?

    Answer: Lunar calendar

    Chinese New Year follows the lunisolar Chinese calendar, meaning the date changes each year between January 21 and February 20.

  8. The Opium Wars were fought between China and which country?

    Answer: Britain

    The Opium Wars (1839-1842 and 1856-1860) were fought between China and Britain over trade disputes, particularly the opium trade.

  9. Which of the following is a characteristic feature of the Chinese 'Dragon Boat Festival' (Duanwu Festival)?

    Answer: Racing dragon boats and eating zongzi

    The Dragon Boat Festival features dragon boat races and eating zongzi (sticky rice dumplings). Mooncakes are associated with the Mid-Autumn Festival, and lantern displays are associated with the Lantern Festival.

  10. The 'Warring States Period' in Chinese history refers to the era before the unification of China under which dynasty?

    Answer: Qin

    The Warring States Period (475-221 BCE) ended when the Qin dynasty, under Qin Shi Huang, conquered all rival states and unified China for the first time.

  11. Which dynasty in China was responsible for building the Forbidden City in Beijing?

    Answer: The Ming Dynasty

    The Forbidden City was constructed during the Ming Dynasty under the Yongle Emperor in the early 1400s and served as the imperial palace for nearly 500 years.

  12. Which of these dynasties ruled China during the time of the Great Wall's major expansion and the Terracotta Army's creation?

    Answer: Qin Dynasty

    The Qin Dynasty (221-206 BCE) was the first to unify China. Its first emperor, Qin Shi Huang, initiated the connection of existing walls into what would become the Great Wall and was buried with the Terracotta Army.

  13. The 'Taiping Rebellion' (1850–1864) was a civil war in China led by:

    Answer: Hong Xiuquan

    Hong Xiuquan, who claimed to be the brother of Jesus Christ, led the Taiping Heavenly Kingdom against the Qing Dynasty.

  14. Which ancient trade route connected China to the Mediterranean region?

    Answer: Silk Road

    The Silk Road was a network of trade routes established during the Han Dynasty, connecting China with Central Asia, the Middle East, and Europe.

  15. Which river, known as the 'Mother River of China', flows through Shanghai?

    Answer: Yangtze River

    The Yangtze, Asia's longest river, flows through major Chinese cities including Shanghai before emptying into the East China Sea.
