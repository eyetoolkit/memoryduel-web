Answer key

All fifteen questions above, with the correct answer and the reasoning.

  1. What is the traditional Japanese New Year's decoration made of pine, bamboo, and plum branches called?

    Answer: Kadomatsu

    Kadomatsu are traditional Japanese decorations placed at the entrance of homes and businesses to welcome ancestral spirits during New Year.

  2. What do people traditionally exchange during the Chinese New Year?

    Answer: Red envelopes

    Red envelopes (hongbao) containing money are exchanged to bring luck and prosperity for the coming year.

  3. In many Western cultures, what is traditionally eaten on January 1st to bring good luck for the new year?

    Answer: Black-eyed peas or lentils

    Legumes like black-eyed peas and lentils resemble coins and are eaten for prosperity; long noodles symbolize longevity in some Asian cultures.

  4. What is the name of the Chinese New Year animal cycle that repeats every 12 years?

    Answer: Chinese zodiac

    The Chinese zodiac features 12 animals (rat, ox, tiger, etc.) cycling over 12 years, deeply influencing Chinese culture.

  5. What is the Japanese New Year tradition of eating 'Osechi Ryori' called in English?

    Answer: New Year food

    Osechi Ryori refers to traditional Japanese New Year foods, each dish with symbolic meaning for health, happiness, and prosperity in the coming year.

  6. Which ancient Persian New Year, marking the beginning of spring, is celebrated with house cleaning and bonfires?

    Answer: Nowruz

    Nowruz is the Persian New Year that marks the beginning of spring and is celebrated with cleaning and bonfires.

  7. What animal is traditionally associated with the Chinese Zodiac?

    Answer: Dragon

    The Chinese Zodiac consists of 12 animals, including the Dragon, Rat, Ox, Tiger, etc.

  8. In which country did the tradition of eating 12 grapes at midnight on New Year's Eve originate?

    Answer: Spain

    The tradition of eating 12 grapes at midnight (una uva por cada campanada) originated in Spain and is now popular across Latin America.

  9. In which country did the tradition of setting off fireworks during New Year celebrations originate?

    Answer: China

    Fireworks originated in ancient China, where they were used to ward off evil spirits during celebrations.

  10. What is the significance of the Chinese 'red envelope' (hóngbāo) tradition?

    Answer: New Year money gifts

    Red envelopes containing money are given during Chinese New Year and other special occasions, symbolizing good luck and blessings.

  11. The 'Lantern Festival' marks the end of which celebration in Chinese culture?

    Answer: Chinese New Year

    The Lantern Festival falls on the 15th day of the first lunar month, signaling the official end of the Chinese New Year celebrations.

  12. During the Japanese New Year (Shogatsu) celebration, what traditional food is often eaten to bring good fortune and longevity?

    Answer: Soba noodles

    Toshikoshi soba (year-crossing noodles) are eaten on New Year's Eve in Japan to symbolize longevity and the cutting away of the old year.

  13. What is the name of the Chinese New Year tradition of giving red envelopes with money?

    Answer: Ang Pao / Hongbao

    Hongbao (红包) red envelopes symbolize good luck and are given during Chinese New Year.

  14. The festival of 'Songkran' marks the New Year in which country?

    Answer: Thailand

    Songkran is the traditional Thai New Year festival. It is famous for its massive water fights, symbolizing the washing away of sins and bad luck from the previous year.

  15. Which festival, originating in ancient Persia, celebrates the spring equinox and the renewal of nature, now celebrated worldwide?

    Answer: Nowruz

    Nowruz, the Persian New Year, marks the spring equinox and has been celebrated for over 3,000 years across Central and West Asia, symbolizing renewal and rebirth.
