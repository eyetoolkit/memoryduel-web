US States Quiz

Most people can recite all fifty states. Fewer can put the right capital next to each one, and almost nobody can do it under pressure. This quiz goes after the part of state knowledge that actually sticks: capitals, abbreviations, borders, and the small set of state facts that turn up in every trivia night.

It runs 24 questions across three difficulty lanes, with no sign-up and no timer unless you want one. Tap an answer and the explanation appears straight away, so a wrong guess becomes a fact you keep rather than a number you forget.

Underneath the quiz is the complete answer key with the reasoning for every question, a list of exactly what the quiz covers, and the questions people ask most about state capitals and abbreviations.

Answer key

All 24 questions, with the correct answer and the reasoning.

  1. How many states are in the United States?

    Answer: 50

    Fifty states, plus the federal district of Columbia and several territories that are not states.

  2. Which state is the largest by area?

    Answer: Alaska

    Alaska is more than twice the size of Texas, which is the second largest state.

  3. Which state is the smallest by area?

    Answer: Rhode Island

    Rhode Island is the smallest state by land area, even though Delaware is the smallest by a narrower margin than most people assume.

  4. What is the capital of California?

    Answer: Sacramento

    Sacramento has been the state capital since 1854. Los Angeles is larger, but size is not how capitals are chosen.

  5. What is the capital of New York?

    Answer: Albany

    Albany has been the capital since 1797. New York City is the largest city in the state but has never been its capital.

  6. What is the capital of Texas?

    Answer: Austin

    Austin became the capital in 1839, when Texas was still an independent republic.

  7. What is the capital of Florida?

    Answer: Tallahassee

    Tallahassee was chosen in 1824 to sit roughly halfway between the two population centres of the territory, St. Augustine and Pensacola.

  8. The Grand Canyon is located in which state?

    Answer: Arizona

    The Grand Canyon lies entirely within Arizona, carved by the Colorado River.

  9. Which state borders exactly one other state?

    Answer: Maine

    Maine touches only New Hampshire. Every other state in the contiguous forty-eight shares a border with at least two.

  10. Which state has the longest coastline?

    Answer: Alaska

    Alaska's coastline is longer than all the other states combined, mostly because of its fjords and thousands of islands.

  11. Which state is known as the Lone Star State?

    Answer: Texas

    The single star on the Texas flag refers to Texas as an independent republic before it joined the Union in 1845.

  12. Which state is known as the Aloha State?

    Answer: Hawaii

    Hawaii adopted the nickname officially in 1959, the year it became a state.

  13. Which state was admitted to the Union most recently?

    Answer: Hawaii

    Hawaii was admitted on 21 August 1959, seven months after Alaska, making it the fiftieth and most recent state.

  14. Which two states lie outside the contiguous United States?

    Answer: Alaska and Hawaii

    Alaska and Hawaii are the only two states that do not share a land border with the rest of the country.

  15. Which four states meet at Four Corners?

    Answer: Arizona, Utah, Colorado, New Mexico

    Four Corners is the only point in the United States where four state boundaries meet.

  16. Mount Rushmore is located in which state?

    Answer: South Dakota

    Mount Rushmore sits in the Black Hills of South Dakota and was carved between 1927 and 1941.

  17. Which state has the smallest population?

    Answer: Wyoming

    Wyoming has the smallest population of any state, at under 600,000 residents, while still ranking tenth in area.

  18. Which river forms the southern border of Texas?

    Answer: Rio Grande

    The Rio Grande separates Texas from Mexico along the state's entire southern edge.

  19. Abraham Lincoln was born in which state?

    Answer: Kentucky

    Lincoln was born in a log cabin in Kentucky in 1809, and later lived in Indiana and Illinois.

  20. Which state produces the most potatoes in the United States?

    Answer: Idaho

    Idaho grows roughly a third of the American potato crop, well ahead of any other state.

  21. Which state name has only one syllable?

    Answer: Maine

    Maine is the only one of the fifty state names that is a single syllable.

  22. Which state is nicknamed the Granite State?

    Answer: New Hampshire

    New Hampshire is the Granite State, named for its granite quarries rather than for any political trait.

  23. Which state was the first to ratify the US Constitution?

    Answer: Delaware

    Delaware ratified on 7 December 1787 and is known as the First State as a result.

  24. Which state has the most national parks?

    Answer: California

    California has nine national parks, one more than Alaska, even though Alaska's parks cover far more land.

What this quiz covers

The questions here are picked for the failure modes people actually have. Anyone can name Texas; far fewer can name its capital without hesitating. Anyone knows Alaska is big; fewer know it also has more coastline than every other state combined, or that it is one of only two states outside the contiguous forty-eight.

Difficulty is mixed on purpose. The easy lane keeps the first few minutes moving, the hard lane is where the quiz separates people, and the answer key explains every one of them — including the ones you got right by elimination.

  • State capitals — including the ones that are deliberately not the biggest city in the state: Sacramento, Albany, Tallahassee, Austin.
  • Postal abbreviations in both directions: CA to California, and California back to CA.
  • The geography that decides borders: which state touches only one other, where Four Corners sits, which state is largest and which is smallest.
  • Records and firsts: the first state to ratify the Constitution, the last state admitted, the state with the smallest population.
  • State nicknames and the facts behind them — Lone Star, Aloha, Granite, First State.

Frequently asked questions

What is the capital of the United States?
Washington, D.C. It is a federal district rather than a state, which is why it does not appear in a list of the fifty state capitals, and why it has no postal abbreviation of the two-letter state kind.
Which state capitals are also the largest city in their state?
Only a minority. Phoenix, Boston, Atlanta, Denver, Indianapolis, Columbus and Oklahoma City are both the capital and the largest city. In most states the capital is a smaller city — Sacramento, Albany and Tallahassee are the classic examples.
How many states are there, and which were the last two admitted?
Fifty. Alaska was admitted on 3 January 1959 and Hawaii on 21 August 1959, which makes Hawaii the most recent state. Before 1959 the count had been forty-eight since 1912.
What is the smallest US state by area?
Rhode Island, at roughly 4,000 square kilometres. Delaware is often guessed, but it is about 1.5 times larger. By population the smallest state is Wyoming.
Does this quiz cover all fifty state capitals?
No. It is 24 questions drawn from a wider pool of state facts, so one run will not hit every state. The answer key lists exactly which ones appeared, which makes it easy to spot the gaps worth revising.
