Answer key

All fifteen questions above, with the correct answer and the reasoning.

  1. Which particle is responsible for mediating the electromagnetic force between charged particles?

    Answer: Photon

    The photon is the massless gauge boson that mediates the electromagnetic interaction. Gluons mediate the strong force, while W and Z bosons mediate the weak force.

  2. What is the Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle about?

    Answer: Simultaneous position and momentum measurement

    It states you cannot precisely know both position and momentum at once.

  3. What is the name of the principle stating that no two electrons in an atom can have the same set of four quantum numbers?

    Answer: Pauli Exclusion Principle

    The Pauli Exclusion Principle dictates that no two identical fermions (like electrons) can occupy the same quantum state simultaneously, which dictates electron shell configurations.

  4. In quantum mechanics, which principle states that you cannot simultaneously know both the exact position and exact momentum of a particle?

    Answer: Heisenberg uncertainty principle

    The Heisenberg uncertainty principle, formulated by Werner Heisenberg, establishes a fundamental limit on how precisely certain pairs of physical properties can be known simultaneously.

  5. Which quantum number describes the shape of an atomic orbital?

    Answer: Azimuthal (ℓ)

    The azimuthal quantum number ℓ determines the orbital shape (s, p, d, f), while n determines energy level.

  6. What principle states that no two identical fermions can occupy the same quantum state simultaneously?

    Answer: Pauli Exclusion Principle

    The Pauli Exclusion Principle explains electron shell structure and the stability of matter.

  7. Which subatomic particle carries the strong nuclear force that binds protons and neutrons together in the nucleus?

    Answer: Gluon

    Gluons are the carrier particles of the strong nuclear force, which holds quarks together within protons and neutrons and binds the nucleus together. Photons carry electromagnetism, electrons are leptons, and neutrinos interact only weakly.

  8. According to quantum mechanics, how many electrons can occupy the n=3 shell in an atom?

    Answer: 18

    The maximum number of electrons in a shell is given by 2n², so for n=3 the capacity is 2·3² = 18 electrons.

  9. What does the Schrödinger equation primarily describe in quantum mechanics?

    Answer: Time evolution of quantum states

    The time-dependent Schrödinger equation describes how quantum states evolve over time, fundamental to understanding atomic and subatomic systems.

  10. According to quantum mechanics, what is the term for the phenomenon where a particle exists in multiple states simultaneously until measured?

    Answer: Superposition

    Superposition allows a quantum system to exist in multiple states until an observation forces it into a definite state.

  11. The Planck constant has units of:

    Answer: A) J·s

    The Planck constant h has dimensions of energy multiplied by time, i.e., joule‑seconds (J·s).

  12. What phenomenon demonstrates that light can behave as both a wave and a particle?

    Answer: Photoelectric effect

    The photoelectric effect, explained by Einstein in 1905, showed light behaves as particles (photons) transferring discrete energy quanta, complementing its wave behavior demonstrated by interference and diffraction.

  13. What does the Pauli Exclusion Principle state about electrons in an atom?

    Answer: No two electrons can have the same set of four quantum numbers

    The Pauli Exclusion Principle states that no two fermions (such as electrons) can occupy the same quantum state simultaneously, which explains electron shell structure.

  14. Which quantum mechanical principle states that the position and momentum of a particle cannot both be precisely known at the same time?

    Answer: Heisenberg uncertainty principle

    The Heisenberg uncertainty principle states that certain pairs of physical properties (like position and momentum) cannot both be known to arbitrary precision. The Pauli exclusion principle relates to identical fermions, Planck's hypothesis relates to quantized energy, and superposition relates to quantum states.

  15. Which scientist proposed the uncertainty principle in quantum mechanics?

    Answer: Werner Heisenberg

    Werner Heisenberg formulated the uncertainty principle, stating that position and momentum cannot both be precisely known simultaneously.
