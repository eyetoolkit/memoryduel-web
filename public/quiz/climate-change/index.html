Answer key

All fifteen questions above, with the correct answer and the reasoning.

  1. What is the main greenhouse gas responsible for anthropogenic climate change?

    Answer: Carbon dioxide

    Carbon dioxide (CO2) is the most abundant greenhouse gas released by human activities such as burning fossil fuels.

  2. Which of the following is a major cause of global warming?

    Answer: The burning of fossil fuels

    Burning fossil fuels like coal, oil, and natural gas releases large amounts of greenhouse gases, primarily carbon dioxide, into the atmosphere, trapping heat and causing global warming.

  3. Which of the following is a major greenhouse gas emitted by human activities?

    Answer: Methane

    Methane (CH4) is a potent greenhouse gas released largely from agriculture and fossil fuel use.

  4. Which human activity contributes most to the increase of atmospheric methane?

    Answer: Rice paddies

    Anaerobic decomposition in rice paddies releases large amounts of methane.

  5. What phenomenon describes the gradual increase in Earth's average temperature over recent decades?

    Answer: Global Warming

    Global warming refers to the long-term heating of Earth's climate system observed since the pre-industrial period (1850-1900), primarily due to human activities increasing greenhouse gas emissions.

  6. What is the 'albedo effect' in environmental science?

    Answer: The reflection of sunlight by Earth's surface

    Albedo is a measure of how much light is reflected by a surface. Bright surfaces (like ice and snow) have a high albedo and reflect sunlight, cooling the planet, while dark surfaces (like oceans and forests) absorb it.

  7. What is the main reason coral reefs are bleaching?

    Answer: Rising sea temperatures

    Coral bleaching primarily occurs when sea temperatures rise, causing corals to expel the symbiotic algae (zooxanthellae) living in their tissues.

  8. The Amazon Rainforest is often referred to as the 'lungs of the Earth.' What percentage of the world's oxygen is it estimated to produce?

    Answer: About 6%

    Despite its reputation, the Amazon produces approximately 6% of the world's oxygen. However, it absorbs massive amounts of CO₂ and is far more critical as a carbon sink than as an oxygen source.

  9. What is the phenomenon known as 'coral bleaching' primarily caused by?

    Answer: Rising ocean temperatures

    Coral bleaching occurs when corals expel the algae (zooxanthellae) living in their tissues due to stress, primarily caused by elevated sea temperatures. Without the algae, the coral loses its color and major source of nutrition.

  10. Which of the following is the primary driver of modern climate change?

    Answer: Human-produced greenhouse gases

    The burning of fossil fuels and other human activities have increased concentrations of greenhouse gases like carbon dioxide, driving modern global warming.

  11. When comparing carbon storage, boreal forests store larger amounts of carbon in which pool compared to tropical rainforests?

    Answer: Soil organic matter

    Boreal soils contain vast stores of organic matter due to slow decomposition rates in cold climates.

  12. Thawing of permafrost can release large amounts of carbon because microbial decomposition of previously frozen organic matter increases when:

    Answer: Temperature rises above freezing

    Warmer temperatures thaw frozen soils, allowing dormant microbes to decompose organic carbon.

  13. What causes ocean acidification?

    Answer: Increased absorption of carbon dioxide

    The ocean absorbs about a quarter of anthropogenic CO2, which reacts with seawater to form carbonic acid, lowering pH and threatening shell-forming organisms.

  14. Which climate feedback loop involves the release of methane from thawing permafrost, leading to further warming?

    Answer: Permafrost carbon‑methane feedback

    As permafrost thaws, trapped organic matter decomposes, releasing methane—a potent greenhouse gas that amplifies warming.

  15. The phenomenon where species shift their geographic ranges toward higher altitudes or latitudes in response to warming temperatures is referred to as a:

    Answer: Species migration

    Climate‑driven range shifts involve organisms moving to cooler areas, commonly called species migration or range shift.
