Answer key

All fifteen questions above, with the correct answer and the reasoning.

  1. What is the term for the variety of life within an area, including genetic, species, and ecosystem diversity?

    Answer: B) Biodiversity

    Biodiversity refers to the variety of life across all levels of biological organization.

  2. What is the largest rainforest in the world?

    Answer: Amazon Rainforest

    The Amazon Rainforest spans much of South America and is the largest tropical rainforest on Earth.

  3. Which animal is known for its black and white coloring and eats bamboo?

    Answer: Giant Panda

    Giant Pandas are native to south central China and have a diet that consists almost entirely of bamboo.

  4. Which of these species is an example of an endangered species?

    Answer: Bengal tiger

    Bengal tigers are endangered due to habitat loss and poaching. The bald eagle has actually recovered from endangered status.

  5. Which of the following is an invasive species known to disrupt ecosystems worldwide?

    Answer: Zebra mussel

    Zebra mussels are a well-known invasive species that clog waterways and outcompete native organisms.

  6. Which animal is known as an 'umbrella species' because protecting it often protects other species in the same habitat?

    Answer: Panda

    The Giant Panda is a classic example of an umbrella species; conserving its bamboo forest habitat protects many other organisms.

  7. Which international agreement sets the target to protect at least 10 % of the world's marine areas?

    Answer: Aichi Biodiversity Target 11 (CBD)

    Aichi Target 11 under the Convention on Biological Diversity aims for 10 % coastal and marine protection.

  8. Beavers are considered ecosystem engineers because they:

    Answer: Create and maintain wetland habitats by building dams

    By constructing dams, beavers alter hydrology, creating diverse wetland environments.

  9. What is the primary cause of the current global decline in insect populations?

    Answer: Habitat loss and pesticide use

    While climate change plays a role, the primary drivers of insect decline are habitat loss (due to urbanization and agriculture) and the widespread use of pesticides.

  10. What phenomenon describes the loss of biodiversity in a specific area due to habitat fragmentation, where small populations become isolated?

    Answer: Island biogeography effect

    The island biogeography effect, applied to habitat fragmentation, describes how isolated habitat patches behave like islands—smaller patches support fewer species and experience higher extinction rates due to reduced gene flow and smaller population sizes.

  11. What is the primary ecological function of a coral reef?

    Answer: To provide habitat and support marine biodiversity

    Coral reefs are often called the 'rainforests of the sea' because they provide complex habitats that support an estimated 25% of all marine species, despite covering less than 1% of the ocean floor.

  12. Which term describes a species that is not native to a particular ecosystem and causes economic or environmental harm?

    Answer: Invasive species

    An invasive species is a non-native organism that spreads rapidly and outcompetes native species, disrupting local ecosystems.

  13. What is the term for the phenomenon where a species' population declines so much that it faces a very high risk of extinction?

    Answer: Endangerment

    Endangerment describes a species at serious risk of extinction in the wild, often due to habitat loss or exploitation.

  14. Which phenomenon occurs when a species' population declines to a point where it can no longer sustain a viable population in the wild?

    Answer: Extinction vortex

    The extinction vortex describes a positive feedback loop where small populations face increasing risks (inbreeding, genetic drift) leading toward extinction.

  15. What is the term for a species that is at risk of extinction due to habitat loss, poaching, or climate change?

    Answer: Threatened Species

    Threatened species are those that are vulnerable to becoming endangered or extinct, often requiring conservation efforts to protect their populations.
