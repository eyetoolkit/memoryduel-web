Answer key

All fifteen questions above, with the correct answer and the reasoning.

  1. Who wrote 'Romeo and Juliet'?

    Answer: William Shakespeare

    William Shakespeare wrote 'Romeo and Juliet', one of his most famous tragedies, in the early 1590s.

  2. What is the name of the boy who never grows up in J.M. Barrie's famous stories?

    Answer: Peter Pan

    Peter Pan is the boy who lives in Neverland and refuses to grow up.

  3. Which famous novel begins with the line 'Call me Ishmael'?

    Answer: Moby-Dick

    The opening line of Herman Melville's 1851 novel 'Moby-Dick' is one of the most famous in English literature.

  4. Which famous book features the character Captain Ahab?

    Answer: Moby-Dick

    Herman Melville's 1851 novel Moby-Dick follows Captain Ahab's obsessive quest to hunt the great white whale. It is considered a masterpiece of American literature.

  5. Which novel features the opening line, 'It was the best of times, it was the worst of times'?

    Answer: A Tale of Two Cities

    This famous opening line belongs to 'A Tale of Two Cities' by Charles Dickens, a historical novel set in London and Paris before and during the French Revolution.

  6. The novel 'To Kill a Mockingbird' was written by which American author?

    Answer: Harper Lee

    Harper Lee wrote 'To Kill a Mockingbird' in 1960. The novel addresses issues of racial injustice in the American South and has become a classic of modern American literature.

  7. Who wrote the play Hamlet?

    Answer: William Shakespeare

    William Shakespeare wrote Hamlet, likely around 1600. It is one of the most performed and quoted plays in the English language.

  8. Who wrote the novel 'Don Quixote', often considered the first modern novel?

    Answer: Miguel de Cervantes

    'Don Quixote' was written by Spanish author Miguel de Cervantes and published in two parts in 1605 and 1615, widely regarded as a foundational work of Western literature.

  9. Who wrote 'The Odyssey'?

    Answer: Homer

    Homer is traditionally credited with composing 'The Odyssey' around the 8th century BCE. The epic poem follows the hero Odysseus on his ten-year journey home after the Trojan War.

  10. Who wrote the classic novel 'Pride and Prejudice'?

    Answer: Jane Austen

    Jane Austen published 'Pride and Prejudice' in 1813. The novel, which explores themes of love, reputation, and class in English society, is one of the most widely read works in English literature.

  11. Who wrote 'The Trial', a novel about a man arrested and prosecuted by a remote authority?

    Answer: Franz Kafka

    Franz Kafka wrote The Trial, published posthumously in 1925; it inspired the term 'Kafkaesque' for surreal, nightmarish bureaucratic situations.

  12. Who wrote the epic poem 'The Divine Comedy,' which describes a journey through Hell, Purgatory, and Paradise?

    Answer: Dante Alighieri

    Dante Alighieri wrote 'The Divine Comedy' in the early 14th century, considered one of the greatest works of world literature and a foundation of the Italian language.

  13. Who wrote 'Crime and Punishment'?

    Answer: Fyodor Dostoevsky

    Fyodor Dostoevsky wrote Crime and Punishment, published in 1866, a profound psychological novel exploring guilt and redemption.

  14. Who wrote the epic novel 'In Search of Lost Time' (also known as 'Remembrance of Things Past')?

    Answer: Marcel Proust

    French author Marcel Proust wrote 'In Search of Lost Time', a seven-volume novel published between 1913 and 1927, considered one of the greatest works of the 20th century.

  15. Which literary work is credited with popularizing the use of the epistolary novel form in English literature with its shipwreck narrative?

    Answer: Robinson Crusoe

    Daniel Defoe's 'Robinson Crusoe' (1719) is often considered one of the first English novels and a pioneering epistolary work.
