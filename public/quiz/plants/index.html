Answer key

All fifteen questions above, with the correct answer and the reasoning.

  1. What is the process by which water evaporates from plants' leaves called?

    Answer: Transpiration

    Transpiration is the release of water vapor through tiny pores called stomata.

  2. What part of a plant absorbs water from the soil?

    Answer: Roots

    Roots absorb water and minerals from the soil and anchor the plant in place.

  3. Which of the following is a primary producer in most ecosystems?

    Answer: Grass

    Primary producers, like grass and other plants, create their own food through photosynthesis, forming the base of the food web.

  4. Which gas do trees primarily absorb from the atmosphere during photosynthesis?

    Answer: Carbon Dioxide

    During photosynthesis, trees and other plants take in carbon dioxide (CO₂) from the air and use sunlight to convert it into glucose and oxygen.

  5. What is the primary function of roots in a plant?

    Answer: To absorb water and minerals

    The main functions of roots are to anchor the plant in the soil and absorb water and dissolved minerals which are then transported to the rest of the plant.

  6. Which of these trees is known for being deciduous and losing its leaves in autumn?

    Answer: Oak

    Oak trees are deciduous, meaning they shed their leaves annually, typically in autumn. Pine, Palm, and Spruce trees are evergreen.

  7. What is the process by which green plants convert sunlight into chemical energy?

    Answer: Photosynthesis

    Photosynthesis is the process by which chlorophyll-containing organisms, such as plants and algae, use sunlight to convert carbon dioxide and water into glucose and oxygen.

  8. What is the process by which a plant grows toward a light source called?

    Answer: Phototropism

    Phototropism is the directional growth of plants in response to light, mediated by auxin redistribution.

  9. Which type of plant produces seeds that are not enclosed in a fruit?

    Answer: Gymnosperms

    Gymnosperms, like conifers, produce 'naked seeds' usually on cones, unlike flowering plants.

  10. Which phenomenon describes the process by which organisms become better suited to their environment over generations?

    Answer: Natural Selection

    Natural selection favors traits that improve survival and reproduction, leading to adaptation over time.

  11. What is the biological term for an organism that can produce its own food using sunlight or chemical energy?

    Answer: Autotroph

    Autotrophs are organisms that produce their own food, typically through photosynthesis (using sunlight) or chemosynthesis (using chemical energy).

  12. What is the biological process where an organism's genetic makeup changes over successive generations, driven by natural selection?

    Answer: Evolution

    Evolution is the gradual change in the heritable characteristics of biological populations over time, primarily driven by natural selection acting on genetic variation.

  13. Which evolutionary adaptation allows desert plants like cacti to minimize water loss?

    Answer: CAM photosynthesis

    CAM (Crassulacean Acid Metabolism) photosynthesis allows cacti to open their stomata at night, reducing water loss while still fixing carbon dioxide.

  14. Which phenomenon, first documented in the Amazon rainforest, describes the ability of some trees to communicate chemical warning signals through underground fungal networks?

    Answer: The Wood Wide Web

    The 'Wood Wide Web' refers to the network of mycorrhizal fungi that connects trees underground, allowing them to share nutrients and send chemical warning signals about threats like insect attacks. This underground network has been extensively studied in old-growth forests.

  15. Which biome is characterized by the lowest levels of precipitation and is home to xerophytic plants?

    Answer: Desert

    Deserts receive very little rainfall (usually less than 25 cm per year). Plants there, called xerophytes, have adapted to retain water, such as cacti.
