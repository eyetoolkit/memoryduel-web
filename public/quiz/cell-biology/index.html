Answer key

All fifteen questions above, with the correct answer and the reasoning.

  1. Which organelle is the site of protein synthesis in a cell?

    Answer: Ribosome

    Ribosomes translate mRNA into polypeptide chains.

  2. What is the powerhouse of the cell?

    Answer: Mitochondria

    Mitochondria are often called the 'powerhouse of the cell' because they generate most of the cell's supply of adenosine triphosphate (ATP), used as a source of chemical energy.

  3. Which organelle is often referred to as the 'powerhouse of the cell'?

    Answer: Mitochondria

    Mitochondria are known as the powerhouse of the cell because they generate most of the cell's supply of adenosine triphosphate (ATP), used as a source of chemical energy.

  4. What is the powerhouse of the cell, responsible for producing most of the cell's supply of adenosine triphosphate (ATP)?

    Answer: Mitochondria

    Mitochondria are organelles found in most eukaryotic cells that generate most of the cell's supply of ATP, the main energy currency.

  5. Which of the following is a primary function of chloroplasts in plant cells?

    Answer: Photosynthesis

    Chloroplasts are the sites of photosynthesis, converting light energy into chemical energy stored in glucose.

  6. What is the powerhouse of the cell that produces energy through cellular respiration?

    Answer: Mitochondria

    Mitochondria generate most of the cell's supply of adenosine triphosphate (ATP), used as a source of chemical energy.

  7. What is the basic structural and functional unit of life found in all living organisms?

    Answer: Cell

    The cell is the smallest unit of life that can replicate independently, and all living things are composed of one or more cells.

  8. Which part of the cell is responsible for generating most of its energy?

    Answer: Mitochondria

    Mitochondria are known as the 'powerhouses' of the cell because they perform cellular respiration, producing ATP (adenosine triphosphate), the cell's main energy currency.

  9. What is the process by which cells divide to form two identical daughter cells?

    Answer: Mitosis

    Mitosis produces two genetically identical daughter cells. Meiosis produces four genetically diverse gametes (sex cells) with half the chromosome number.

  10. What is the smallest unit of life capable of independent existence?

    Answer: Cell

    The cell is the basic structural and functional unit of all living organisms.

  11. Which organelle contains the cell's genetic material in eukaryotic cells?

    Answer: Nucleus

    The nucleus houses DNA and controls cellular activities through gene expression.

  12. Which organelle is responsible for photosynthesis in plant cells?

    Answer: Chloroplast

    Chloroplasts contain chlorophyll, the green pigment that captures light energy for photosynthesis. Mitochondria produce energy through respiration, vacuoles store substances, and lysosomes break down waste.

  13. In biological terms, what is the primary role of ribosomes within a cell?

    Answer: To synthesize proteins

    Ribosomes are the cellular structures responsible for protein synthesis (translation), reading messenger RNA (mRNA) and assembling amino acids into polypeptide chains. DNA stores genetic info, mitochondria produce ATP, and lysosomes break down waste.

  14. What is the powerhouse molecule that cells use to store and transfer energy?

    Answer: ATP

    Adenosine triphosphate (ATP) is the primary energy carrier in cells.

  15. What is the process by which a cell engulfs a large particle or another cell by wrapping it with its cell membrane?

    Answer: Endocytosis

    Endocytosis is a type of active transport in which a cell transports molecules (such as proteins) into the cell by engulfing them in an energy-using process. Phagocytosis, the engulfing of large particles, is a form of endocytosis.
