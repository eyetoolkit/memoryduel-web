Periodic Table Quiz

The periodic table rewards two different kinds of memory. There is the lookup kind — Fe is iron, Au is gold, atomic number 6 is carbon — and there is the structural kind, where you know why sodium reacts and argon does not, without having to recall a single row of the table.

This quiz tests both. 22 questions across symbols, atomic numbers, group names and periodic trends, with an explanation after every answer so that a wrong guess turns into a rule rather than a fact.

Answer key

All 22 questions, with the correct answer and the reasoning.

  1. What is the chemical symbol for gold?

    Answer: Au

    Gold’s symbol comes from the Latin 'aurum' and is Au.

  2. What is the atomic number of carbon?

    Answer: 6

    Carbon’s nucleus contains 6 protons, giving it atomic number 6.

  3. Which element has the highest electronegativity value on the Pauling scale?

    Answer: Fluorine

    Fluorine has the highest electronegativity (≈3.98) of all elements.

  4. Which element has the chemical symbol 'O'?

    Answer: Oxygen

    The chemical symbol 'O' corresponds to the element oxygen, atomic number 8.

  5. In the periodic table, which group contains the noble gases?

    Answer: Group 18

    Noble gases are located in Group 18 (formerly Group 0), characterized by full valence electron shells.

  6. Which element has the highest known melting point?

    Answer: Tungsten

    Tungsten melts at 3,422 °C (6,192 °F), the highest melting point among all elements under standard pressure.

  7. What is the chemical symbol for sodium?

    Answer: Na

    Sodium's chemical symbol is Na, derived from its Latin name 'natrium'.

  8. Which of the following is a noble gas?

    Answer: Argon

    Argon (Ar) belongs to the noble gases, a group of inert elements that rarely form chemical compounds.

  9. Which everyday object contains the highest number of distinct chemical elements?

    Answer: Smartphone

    Modern smartphones incorporate more than 60 different elements, including rare earth metals, making them richer in distinct elements than the human body or simple objects.

  10. Which chemical element is the most abundant in Earth's crust?

    Answer: Oxygen

    Oxygen makes up roughly 46 % of the crust by weight, the most abundant element.

  11. Which element has the ground‑state electron configuration [Ar] 4s² 3d⁶?

    Answer: Fe

    Iron (Fe) has atomic number 26, giving the configuration [Ar]4s²3d⁶.

  12. Which element is liquid at room temperature apart from mercury?

    Answer: Bromine

    Bromine is a red-brown liquid at room temperature. Chlorine is a gas and iodine a solid at the same temperature.

  13. Who published the first widely accepted periodic table, in 1869?

    Answer: Dmitri Mendeleev

    Mendeleev ordered the known elements by atomic weight and left gaps for elements that had not yet been discovered.

  14. In a neutral atom, what is the relationship between the number of protons and electrons?

    Answer: Protons = electrons

    A neutral atom has equal numbers of positive protons and negative electrons.

  15. How many periods does the standard periodic table have?

    Answer: Seven

    Seven periods, corresponding to the filling of the electron shells. Period 7 is the one that was completed in 2016.

  16. In a chemical reaction, which subatomic particle is transferred when an ion forms?

    Answer: Electron

    Ion formation involves the gain or loss of electrons, not protons or neutrons.

  17. Which gas is the lightest element and the most abundant in the universe?

    Answer: Hydrogen

    Hydrogen has the lowest atomic mass of any element and is the primary component of stars.

  18. Which element has the atomic number 79?

    Answer: Gold

    Gold's atomic number is 79, placing it among the transition metals on the periodic table.

  19. Which element has the chemical symbol 'Na'?

    Answer: Sodium

    'Na' is the modern abbreviation for sodium, derived from Latin natrium.

  20. Which element has the highest first ionization energy among Na, Mg, Al, and Si?

    Answer: Si

    First ionization energy generally increases across a period; Si, being furthest right in this set, has the highest value.

  21. Which group of the periodic table contains the alkali metals?

    Answer: Group 1

    Group 1 starts with lithium, sodium and potassium. All of them have a single outer electron, which is why they react so readily.

  22. What is the chemical symbol for lead?

    Answer: Pb

    Pb comes from the Latin plumbum, which is also where the word plumbing comes from.

What this quiz covers

Half of these questions can be answered by pure recall and half require a rule. That split is deliberate: the table is not a list to memorise but a map with directions, and the questions in the hard lane are the ones where the map matters more than the list.

Where a question depends on a trend, the explanation names the reason — shielding, nuclear charge, distance from the nucleus — instead of just restating the answer, because the reason is what transfers to the next question.

  • Symbols in both directions: Au to gold, and gold back to Au — including the ones that do not match the English name (Na, K, Fe, Pb, Hg, W).
  • Atomic numbers and what they actually count: protons, which define the element.
  • Groups and families: noble gases in group 18, alkali metals in group 1, halogens in group 17.
  • Periodic trends: electronegativity, melting point and first ionisation energy, and which corner of the table they point towards.
  • A handful of structural facts — who published the table, how many periods it has, and which elements are liquid at room temperature.

Frequently asked questions

How many elements are in the periodic table?
118, arranged in seven periods and eighteen groups. Elements 113 to 118 were formally named between 2016 and 2017, which completed the seventh period.
Who created the periodic table?
Dmitri Mendeleev published the version that became standard in 1869. He ordered the known elements by atomic weight, left gaps for elements not yet discovered, and predicted their properties — several of those predictions were later confirmed.
What is the difference between atomic number and mass number?
Atomic number is the number of protons in the nucleus and defines which element it is. Mass number is protons plus neutrons, so two atoms of the same element with different mass numbers are isotopes.
Why are noble gases so unreactive?
Their outer electron shell is full, so they have no energetic reason to gain, lose or share electrons. That is why helium, neon and argon are used where a chemically inert atmosphere is needed.
Which elements are liquid at room temperature?
Mercury and bromine. Gallium, caesium and francium melt at slightly above room temperature, so they are solid in most rooms but liquid on a warm day.
