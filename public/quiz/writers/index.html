Answer key

All fifteen questions above, with the correct answer and the reasoning.

  1. Who wrote the fantasy series 'Harry Potter'?

    Answer: J.K. Rowling

    J.K. Rowling is the British author of the Harry Potter series, which has become the best-selling book series in history.

  2. Who wrote 'Romeo and Juliet'?

    Answer: William Shakespeare

    William Shakespeare wrote 'Romeo and Juliet' around 1594–1596. It remains one of the most performed plays worldwide.

  3. In which city did Shakespeare's plays first perform?

    Answer: London

    William Shakespeare's plays were performed in London, primarily at the famous Globe Theatre, beginning in the late 1590s.

  4. Which famous novel begins with the line 'Call me Ishmael'?

    Answer: Moby-Dick

    'Moby-Dick' by Herman Melville, published in 1851, opens with one of the most famous first lines in literature: 'Call me Ishmael.'

  5. Who wrote the classic novel 'To Kill a Mockingbird'?

    Answer: Harper Lee

    Harper Lee published 'To Kill a Mockingbird' in 1960. The novel won the Pulitzer Prize and deals with racial injustice in the American South through the eyes of young Scout Finch.

  6. Who wrote the novel '1984', which introduced the term 'Big Brother' to popular culture?

    Answer: George Orwell

    George Orwell published '1984' in 1949, creating the dystopian concept of 'Big Brother' and terms like 'thoughtcrime' and 'Newspeak'.

  7. Which Russian author wrote 'Crime and Punishment'?

    Answer: Fyodor Dostoevsky

    Fyodor Dostoevsky published 'Crime and Punishment' in 1866. The novel follows the psychological journey of a murderer and is considered a masterpiece of world literature.

  8. Which author wrote 'One Hundred Years of Solitude'?

    Answer: Gabriel García Márquez

    Gabriel García Márquez, a Colombian novelist, wrote 'One Hundred Years of Solitude', a landmark of magical realism published in 1967.

  9. Which literary genre is Ray Bradbury's 'Fahrenheit 451' primarily associated with?

    Answer: Science Fiction

    'Fahrenheit 451', published in 1953, is a classic of dystopian science fiction, depicting a future society where books are banned.

  10. Who wrote the play 'A Doll's House'?

    Answer: Henrik Ibsen

    Norwegian playwright Henrik Ibsen wrote 'A Doll's House' in 1879. It is considered a groundbreaking work that challenged 19th-century marriage and gender norms.

  11. Which Japanese author wrote the novel 'Norwegian Wood', a bestselling coming-of-age story published in 1987?

    Answer: Haruki Murakami

    Haruki Murakami's 'Norwegian Wood' became a massive bestseller in Japan and internationally. Unlike his more surreal works, it is a realistic novel dealing with loss, nostalgia, and sexuality in 1960s Tokyo.

  12. Which Japanese author won the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1994?

    Answer: Kenzaburō Ōe

    Kenzaburō Ōe won the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1994 for his powerful novels dealing with existential questions.

  13. Who wrote 'Things Fall Apart', a novel about colonialism in Nigeria?

    Answer: Chinua Achebe

    Things Fall Apart (1958) is a celebrated novel by Nigerian author Chinua Achebe, depicting pre-colonial life and the impact of colonialism.

  14. Who wrote the epic poem 'Paradise Lost'?

    Answer: John Milton

    John Milton's 'Paradise Lost' is a 17th-century epic poem in blank verse about the biblical story of the Fall of Man.

  15. Which novel by Gabriel García Márquez is considered a masterpiece of magical realism?

    Answer: One Hundred Years of Solitude

    'One Hundred Years of Solitude' is widely regarded as Gabriel García Márquez's masterpiece and a defining text of magical realism, published in 1967.
