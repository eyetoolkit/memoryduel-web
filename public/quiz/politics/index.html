Answer key

All fifteen questions above, with the correct answer and the reasoning.

  1. Which country was the first to be granted independence from the British Empire in modern times?

    Answer: United States

    While the exact date is debated, the United States declared independence in 1776 and fought the Revolutionary War, becoming the first major colony to gain independence from Britain.

  2. Which event marked the beginning of World War II in Europe?

    Answer: Invasion of Poland

    Germany's invasion of Poland on September 1, 1939, prompted Britain and France to declare war, starting WWII in Europe.

  3. What was the primary language spoken in the Roman Empire?

    Answer: Latin

    Latin was the official language of the Roman administration and the basis for the later Romance languages.

  4. Who was the first Emperor of Rome?

    Answer: Augustus

    Augustus (born Gaius Octavius) became the first Roman emperor following the assassination of Julius Caesar, marking the start of the Roman Empire.

  5. Which empire was ruled by Julius Caesar and Augustus?

    Answer: Roman Empire

    Julius Caesar and his successor Augustus were pivotal figures in the rise of the Roman Empire.

  6. Who was the first woman to lead a modern independent African nation?

    Answer: Ellen Johnson Sirleaf

    Ellen Johnson Sirleaf became the first elected female head of state in Africa when she became President of Liberia in 2006.

  7. The 'Iron Curtain' was a term used to describe the political division of which continent during the Cold War?

    Answer: Europe

    Popularized by Winston Churchill, the 'Iron Curtain' described the ideological and physical boundary dividing Europe into Soviet and Western blocs.

  8. The Berlin Wall, a symbol of the Cold War, fell in which year?

    Answer: 1989

    The Berlin Wall fell on November 9, 1989, leading to German reunification the following year.

  9. The Cold War was primarily a geopolitical rivalry between which two blocs?

    Answer: NATO and the Warsaw Pact

    The Cold War pitted the Western bloc (led by the US/NATO) against the Eastern bloc (led by the USSR/Warsaw Pact).

  10. The policy of 'Glasnost', introduced in the 1980s, was associated with the leader of which country?

    Answer: Soviet Union

    Glasnost (meaning 'openness') was a policy of increased transparency and freedom of information introduced by Soviet leader Mikhail Gorbachev.

  11. The partition of India in 1947 resulted in the creation of which two nations?

    Answer: India and Pakistan

    In 1947, British India was partitioned into two independent dominions: Hindu-majority India and Muslim-majority Pakistan (which included East Pakistan, later Bangladesh).

  12. The Congress of Vienna, held in 1814–1815, aimed to redraw the map of Europe after which event?

    Answer: The Napoleonic Wars

    The Congress of Vienna sought to restore stability and the balance of power in Europe after Napoleon's defeat. It redrew borders and established a framework that maintained relative peace for decades.

  13. The 'Meiji Restoration' of 1868 marked the return of power to the Emperor of which country, ending centuries of feudal military rule?

    Answer: Japan

    The Meiji Restoration ended the Tokugawa Shogunate and restored imperial rule to Japan, ushering in a period of rapid modernization and industrialization known as the Meiji era.

  14. The policy of 'Apartheid' was an official system of racial segregation in which country during the mid-to-late 20th century?

    Answer: South Africa

    Apartheid was a system of institutionalized racial segregation that existed in South Africa from 1948 until the early 1990s.

  15. Who was the principal architect of the European Union's Maastricht Treaty?

    Answer: Jacques Delors

    Jacques Delors, as President of the European Commission, played a key role in negotiating the Maastricht Treaty, which established the European Union.
