Answer key

All fifteen questions above, with the correct answer and the reasoning.

  1. In which year did Christopher Columbus first reach the Americas?

    Answer: 1492

    Christopher Columbus's first voyage, sponsored by Spain, made landfall in the Caribbean in October 1492, initiating sustained European contact with the Americas.

  2. In which year did World War II end in Europe?

    Answer: 1945

    Germany signed unconditional surrender on May 8, 1945 (V-E Day), ending the war in Europe; the Pacific war ended later that year.

  3. Which war was fought between the Allied Powers and the Central Powers from 1914 to 1918?

    Answer: World War I

    World War I (1914–1918) pitted the Allies (Britain, France, Russia, later the US) against the Central Powers (Germany, Austria-Hungary, Ottoman Empire).

  4. The Great Depression began in which country?

    Answer: United States

    The Great Depression began in the United States in 1929 with the stock market crash, then spread worldwide.

  5. The Meiji Restoration in 1868 brought power to which country's emperor?

    Answer: Japan

    The Meiji Restoration of 1868 restored imperial rule to Japan under Emperor Meiji and began rapid modernization and Westernization.

  6. The Taiping Rebellion, one of the deadliest conflicts in history, took place in which country?

    Answer: China

    The Taiping Rebellion (1850-1864) in China was a massive civil war led by Hong Xiuquan, causing an estimated 20-30 million deaths.

  7. Which battle in 1805 confirmed British naval dominance for over a century?

    Answer: Battle of Trafalgar

    The Battle of Trafalgar in 1805, where Admiral Nelson defeated the combined French and Spanish fleets, established British naval supremacy.

  8. The Cold War was primarily a geopolitical tension between which two superpowers?

    Answer: United States and Soviet Union

    The Cold War (c. 1947–1991) was the prolonged geopolitical tension between the United States and the Soviet Union, fought through proxy wars, nuclear arms races, and ideological competition.

  9. Who was the leader of the Indian independence movement who advocated non-violent civil disobedience?

    Answer: Mahatma Gandhi

    Mahatma Gandhi led the movement using non-violent resistance to gain independence from Britain in 1947.

  10. Which war was fought between 1950 and 1953 on the Korean Peninsula?

    Answer: Korean War

    The Korean War was fought from 1950 to 1953 between North Korea (supported by China and the Soviet Union) and South Korea (supported by the United Nations, led by the U.S.).

  11. Which treaty ended World War I and imposed heavy reparations on Germany?

    Answer: Treaty of Versailles

    The Treaty of Versailles, signed in 1919, ended World War I and imposed significant territorial losses and reparations on Germany.

  12. Which event is considered the immediate trigger of World War I?

    Answer: Assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand

    The assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand of Austria-Hungary on June 28, 1914, in Sarajevo triggered a chain of alliances that led to World War I.

  13. In 1961, the Berlin Wall was constructed to stop the exodus of East Germans to the West. Who was the leader of East Germany (the GDR) at the time?

    Answer: Walter Ulbricht

    Walter Ulbricht was the leader of the Socialist Unity Party of Germany (SED) and the head of state of the GDR when the wall was built.

  14. The Taiping Rebellion (1850–1864) in China was led by which self-proclaimed messiah figure?

    Answer: Hong Xiuquan

    Hong Xiuquan led the Taiping Rebellion, a massive civil war in China that resulted in 20-30 million deaths, claiming to be the brother of Jesus Christ.

  15. Which 19th-century ideology sought to unite the scattered Italian-speaking states into a single nation?

    Answer: Risorgimento

    The Risorgimento ('Resurgence') was the political and social movement that consolidated various states of the Italian peninsula into the Kingdom of Italy by 1861.
