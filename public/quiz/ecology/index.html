Answer key

All fifteen questions above, with the correct answer and the reasoning.

  1. Which of the following is considered a biotic factor in an ecosystem?

    Answer: Plants

    Biotic factors are living components; plants are living organisms within an ecosystem.

  2. Which gas do plants primarily absorb from the atmosphere during photosynthesis?

    Answer: Carbon Dioxide

    Plants absorb carbon dioxide (CO₂) from the air and use sunlight energy to convert it into glucose and oxygen through photosynthesis.

  3. Which of these is NOT a type of habitat?

    Answer: Hedgehog

    A hedgehog is an animal. Rainforests, deserts, and reefs are habitats where organisms live.

  4. Which of these ecosystems receives the most annual rainfall?

    Answer: Tropical rainforest

    Tropical rainforests are characterized by high precipitation, often exceeding 2000 mm per year.

  5. What is the process called when green plants use sunlight to make their own food?

    Answer: Photosynthesis

    Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants and some other organisms convert sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide into glucose and oxygen.

  6. What is the primary role of decomposers in an ecosystem?

    Answer: Break down dead organic matter

    Decomposers such as fungi and bacteria break down dead organic material, recycling nutrients back into the soil.

  7. What phenomenon describes the expansion of a species into new geographic areas?

    Answer: Colonization

    Colonization occurs when a species establishes itself in a new area previously unoccupied by it.

  8. What is the term for animals that only eat plants?

    Answer: Herbivore

    Herbivores such as deer and cows feed exclusively on plant material.

  9. What is a 'mycorrhizal network' often referred to as in forestry?

    Answer: The 'Wood Wide Web'

    Mycorrhizal networks are underground fungal networks that connect the roots of different plants, allowing them to share nutrients and chemical signals.

  10. In the nitrogen cycle, which process converts atmospheric nitrogen into a form usable by plants?

    Answer: Nitrogen fixation

    Nitrogen fixation, carried out by certain bacteria and archaea, reduces atmospheric N₂ to ammonia (NH₃) or related ions that plants can assimilate.

  11. Which biome is characterized by low temperatures, low precipitation, and vegetation consisting primarily of mosses, lichens, and dwarf shrubs?

    Answer: Tundra

    The Tundra is a treeless biome found in the Arctic and on the tops of mountains, where permafrost is present and the growing season is short.

  12. Which biogeochemical cycle is most closely associated with the nitrogen-fixing bacteria in root nodules of legumes?

    Answer: Nitrogen cycle

    Nitrogen-fixing bacteria convert atmospheric nitrogen into ammonia that plants can use, playing a key role in the nitrogen cycle.

  13. Which of the following terms describes the maximum population size of a species that a particular environment can sustain indefinitely?

    Answer: Carrying capacity

    Carrying capacity (K) is the maximum number of individuals of a given species that an environment can support over time without degrading the resource base, as described by the logistic growth model.

  14. Which ecological concept describes a situation where one species benefits at the expense of another, without necessarily killing it?

    Answer: Parasitism

    Parasitism is a symbiotic relationship where the parasite benefits at the host's expense; the host is typically harmed but not immediately killed.

  15. What is the name of the biogeochemical cycle that moves nitrogen through the atmosphere, soil, and living organisms?

    Answer: Nitrogen cycle

    The nitrogen cycle involves nitrogen fixation, nitrification, assimilation, ammonification, and denitrification, moving nitrogen through various forms.
