Answer key

All fifteen questions above, with the correct answer and the reasoning.

  1. Which classical composer wrote the famous symphony 'Für Elise'?

    Answer: Ludwig van Beethoven

    'Für Elise' is a famous bagatelle composed by Ludwig van Beethoven for solo piano.

  2. Who composed 'Für Elise'?

    Answer: Ludwig van Beethoven

    'Für Elise' (Bagatelle No. 25 in A minor) is one of the most famous works by Ludwig van Beethoven.

  3. Who composed the opera "The Marriage of Figaro"?

    Answer: Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart

    Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart composed this famous opera in 1786, based on a play by Pierre Beaumarchais. It remains one of the most performed operas in the world.

  4. Who composed the symphony known as the "Eroica"?

    Answer: Ludwig van Beethoven

    Beethoven's Symphony No. 3 in E-flat major, Op. 55, nicknamed "Eroica" (Heroic), was composed around 1803-1804.

  5. Which artist composed the famous piano piece 'Clair de Lune'?

    Answer: Claude Debussy

    'Clair de Lune' is the third movement of Debussy's 'Suite bergamasque,' composed around 1905. It is one of the most recognizable pieces in the impressionist repertoire.

  6. Which Norwegian composer wrote the incidental music piece 'In the Hall of the Mountain King'?

    Answer: Edvard Grieg

    Grieg wrote 'In the Hall of the Mountain King' as part of the incidental music for Henrik Ibsen's play 'Peer Gynt'.

  7. Which composer wrote the ballets 'Swan Lake', 'The Sleeping Beauty', and 'The Nutcracker'?

    Answer: Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky

    Tchaikovsky composed all three famous ballets. 'The Nutcracker' in particular is performed worldwide every winter holiday season.

  8. Which Austrian composer was profoundly deaf for much of his later life but continued to compose masterworks?

    Answer: Ludwig van Beethoven

    Beethoven began losing his hearing in his late twenties and was almost completely deaf by the time he composed his Ninth Symphony, yet his output remained groundbreaking.

  9. Which famous composer continued composing music after becoming completely deaf?

    Answer: Beethoven

    Beethoven began losing his hearing in his late twenties and was almost totally deaf by the time he composed his famous Ninth Symphony.

  10. Which composer's works include 'The Rite of Spring,' which caused a riot at its 1913 premiere?

    Answer: Igor Stravinsky

    Igor Stravinsky's 'The Rite of Spring' (Le Sacre du printemps) premiered in Paris on May 29, 1913. Its avant-garde rhythms and dissonances provoked a near-riot in the audience, making it one of the most notorious events in music history.

  11. Which composer's 'Requiem in D minor, K. 626' was left unfinished at his death in 1791?

    Answer: Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart

    Mozart's Requiem was commissioned anonymously and completed partly by his student Franz Xaver Süssmayr.

  12. Which composer, nicknamed 'The Red Priest' due to his red hair, wrote 'Summer' from 'The Four Seasons'?

    Answer: Antonio Vivaldi

    Antonio Vivaldi was an ordained Catholic priest and had red hair, earning him the nickname 'Il Prete Rosso' (The Red Priest).

  13. Which of these composers was blind later in life while composing his most famous works, such as 'The Messiah'?

    Answer: George Frideric Handel

    George Frideric Handel went blind later in life, though he continued to compose. (Note: Option 1 is a distractor spelling).

  14. Which composer of the 'Well-Tempered Clavier' is often credited with shaping the development of Western music through the Baroque era?

    Answer: Johann Sebastian Bach

    Johann Sebastian Bach composed 'The Well-Tempered Clavier', a foundational work in Western classical music consisting of preludes and fugues in all major and minor keys.

  15. Which classical work begins with the famous four-note motif often called 'fate knocking at the door'?

    Answer: Symphony No. 5 by Beethoven

    Beethoven's Fifth Symphony opens with the iconic short-short-short-long motif; Beethoven reportedly described it as 'fate knocking at the door'.
