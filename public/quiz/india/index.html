Answer key

All fifteen questions above, with the correct answer and the reasoning.

  1. Which religion is the most widely practiced in India?

    Answer: Hinduism

    Hinduism is practiced by roughly 80% of India's population, making it the country's dominant religion.

  2. Which festival is known as the 'Festival of Lights' and celebrates the victory of light over darkness in Hindu tradition?

    Answer: Diwali

    Diwali (Deepavali) is the Hindu festival of lights celebrated across India and by Hindus worldwide. It signifies the victory of light over darkness, good over evil, and knowledge over ignorance through various mythological stories.

  3. Which festival is known as the 'Festival of Lights' and is celebrated by Hindus, Sikhs, and Jains around the world?

    Answer: Diwali

    Diwali, also called Deepavali, is the festival of lights symbolizing the victory of light over darkness and good over evil. It is celebrated by multiple religions in India and by diaspora communities worldwide.

  4. In which country is the festival of Diwali primarily celebrated with fireworks, oil lamps, and sweets?

    Answer: India

    Diwali, the festival of lights, is most widely celebrated in India, where families light oil lamps and set off fireworks.

  5. What is the name of the Hindu spring festival celebrated by throwing colored powders at one another?

    Answer: Holi

    Holi, also known as the 'Festival of Colors,' celebrates the arrival of spring and the triumph of good over evil.

  6. Who led the salt march in India in 1930 as an act of nonviolent civil disobedience?

    Answer: Mahatma Gandhi

    Gandhi's Dandi March challenged the British salt monopoly and became a major symbol of the Indian independence movement.

  7. The festival of Holi, known as the 'Festival of Colors', is celebrated in which South Asian country?

    Answer: India

    While celebrated across South Asia (including Nepal and Pakistan), Holi is most prominently and widely associated with India, where it is a major public holiday.

  8. The partition of India in 1947 created which two countries?

    Answer: India and Pakistan

    British India was partitioned in 1947 into Hindu-majority India and Muslim-majority Pakistan (which included present-day Bangladesh until 1971).

  9. Who led the Indian independence movement through nonviolent resistance and fasts?

    Answer: Mahatma Gandhi

    Mohandas K. Gandhi led the Indian independence movement through campaigns of nonviolent civil disobedience (satyagraha), including the Salt March of 1930.

  10. Which river did Alexander the Great's army refuse to cross in 326 BCE?

    Answer: Hyphasis River

    Alexander the Great's army mutinied at the Hyphasis River (modern-day Beas River in India) and refused to continue east, marking the easternmost limit of his conquests.

  11. Which Bengali festival, honoring the goddess Durga during the autumn months, features elaborate pandals, cultural performances, and the ritual immersion of idols?

    Answer: Durga Puja

    Durga Puja is a major West Bengal celebration of Durga's victory over buffalo demon Mahishasura.

  12. Which civilization is credited with inventing the concept of zero in mathematics?

    Answer: Indian

    Indian mathematicians, particularly Brahmagupta in the 7th century CE, developed the concept of zero as a number, which later spread to the Islamic world and then to Europe.

  13. What is the 'Kumkum' or 'Bindi' often applied to the forehead in Hindu culture meant to represent?

    Answer: The third eye

    In Hindu tradition, the Bindi or Kumkum represents the 'third eye' (Ajna Chakra), the seat of concealed wisdom.

  14. The ancient festival of Upacara, specifically the 'Season of Plenty' ceremony, is a harvest festival celebrated by the Khasi people in which country?

    Answer: India

    The Ka Pem (Season of Plenty) or other Khasi harvest festivals are celebrated in the state of Meghalaya, India, reflecting their deep connection to agriculture.

  15. The concept of 'Jugaad' in India refers to a specific cultural approach to problem-solving best described as:

    Answer: A frugal, innovative, and flexible workaround

    Jugaad is a Hindi word that means a 'hack' or a 'workaround.' It represents a flexible approach to problem-solving that uses limited resources in an innovative way, a highly valued trait in Indian culture.
