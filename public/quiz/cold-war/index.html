Answer key

All fifteen questions above, with the correct answer and the reasoning.

  1. Which wall was torn down in 1989, symbolizing the end of the Cold War?

    Answer: Berlin Wall

    The Berlin Wall fell on November 9, 1989, symbolizing the end of the Cold War division between East and West Germany.

  2. The Berlin Wall, a symbol of the Cold War, was built in which year?

    Answer: 1961

    The Berlin Wall was constructed by the German Democratic Republic (East Germany) in August 1961 to prevent East Germans from fleeing to the West. It fell in 1989.

  3. Which wall symbolized the division of East and West Germany during the Cold War?

    Answer: The Berlin Wall

    The Berlin Wall stood from 1961 to 1989, physically and ideologically dividing Berlin.

  4. The 'Iron Curtain' term was popularized in a 1946 speech by which U.S. president?

    Answer: Harry S. Truman

    The phrase 'Iron Curtain' was coined by Winston Churchill, but it was U.S. President Harry S. Truman who popularized its use in American political discourse during the early Cold War, particularly in his 1947 Truman Doctrine speech.

  5. Which country was the first to successfully put a human into space in 1961?

    Answer: Soviet Union (USSR)

    Soviet cosmonaut Yuri Gagarin became the first human in space aboard Vostok 1 in April 1961.

  6. The 'Cold War' refers to the geopolitical tension between which two superpowers?

    Answer: USA and Soviet Union

    The Cold War was primarily a standoff between the United States and the Soviet Union from the end of WWII until 1991.

  7. The fall of the Berlin Wall in 1989 symbolized the end of which era?

    Answer: The Cold War

    The fall of the Berlin Wall on November 9, 1989, marked the collapse of communist regimes in Eastern Europe and the beginning of the end of the Cold War.

  8. Which war was fought between 1950 and 1953 on the Korean Peninsula?

    Answer: Korean War

    The Korean War was fought between North and South Korea from 1950 to 1953, involving United Nations and Chinese forces.

  9. Which Cold War event saw a confrontation between US and Soviet missiles in Cuba in 1962?

    Answer: Cuban Missile Crisis

    The crisis brought the world close to nuclear war until both sides agreed to withdraw missiles.

  10. In the board game 'Twilight Struggle', what is the die roll modifier for the 'Space Race' event when the United States attempts a manned lunar landing in Turn 8?

    Answer: +3

    In Twilight Struggle, the US receives a +3 modification to the die roll for a lunar‑landing attempt on Turn 8.

  11. Which 1962 event brought the world closest to nuclear war during the Cold War?

    Answer: Cuban Missile Crisis

    The Cuban Missile Crisis in October 1962 was a 13-day confrontation between the US and USSR over Soviet missiles in Cuba.

  12. The Cold War 'policy' of containment was primarily associated with which U.S. President's foreign policy doctrine?

    Answer: Harry S. Truman

    The Truman Doctrine, announced in 1947, established the policy of containment to stop the spread of communism, guiding U.S. foreign policy for decades.

  13. Which 20th-century leader championed the 'Non-Aligned Movement' during the Cold War?

    Answer: Jawaharlal Nehru

    India's Jawaharlal Nehru was a founding leader of the Non-Aligned Movement, which sought to keep newly independent nations neutral between the US and Soviet blocs during the Cold War.

  14. In 1961, the Berlin Wall was constructed to stop the exodus of East Germans to the West. Who was the leader of East Germany (the GDR) at the time?

    Answer: Walter Ulbricht

    Walter Ulbricht was the leader of the Socialist Unity Party of Germany (SED) and the head of state of the GDR when the wall was built.

  15. Which 1955 conference in Bandung, Indonesia was significant for non-aligned nations?

    Answer: Bandung Conference

    The Bandung Conference brought together Asian and African nations, laying groundwork for the Non-Aligned Movement.
