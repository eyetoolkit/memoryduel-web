Answer key

All fifteen questions above, with the correct answer and the reasoning.

  1. In which religion is the concept of Dharma a central principle?

    Answer: Hinduism

    Dharma is a complex concept in Hinduism and other Indian religions that relates to cosmic law, social duty, and morality.

  2. Which philosopher is known as the founder of Western philosophy?

    Answer: Socrates

    Socrates is widely regarded as the founder of Western philosophy, known for the Socratic method of questioning.

  3. Which major world religion is known for its emphasis on the 'Four Noble Truths' and the 'Eightfold Path'?

    Answer: Buddhism

    Buddhism, founded by Siddhartha Gautama (the Buddha), centers on the Four Noble Truths (the nature of suffering) and the Noble Eightfold Path as the way to end suffering and achieve enlightenment.

  4. Which religion is based on the teachings of the Buddha?

    Answer: Buddhism

    Buddhism originated with Siddhartha Gautama (the Buddha) and focuses on the path to enlightenment through the Four Noble Truths.

  5. What does the Chinese 'Yin and Yang' symbol represent?

    Answer: Balance and harmony

    Yin and Yang represent the concept of dualism, describing how seemingly opposite forces may actually be complementary.

  6. What is the Japanese concept of finding beauty in imperfection and transience, often associated with cherry blossoms?

    Answer: Wabi-sabi

    Wabi-sabi is a Japanese worldview centered on the acceptance of transience and imperfection, often linked to the fleeting beauty of cherry blossoms.

  7. Who wrote the Communist Manifesto with Friedrich Engels?

    Answer: Karl Marx

    Karl Marx co-authored the Communist Manifesto in 1848, a foundational text of communism.

  8. In which religion is the concept of 'Nirvana' a primary goal?

    Answer: Buddhism

    Nirvana is a central concept in Buddhism, referring to the extinguishing of suffering and the cycle of rebirth.

  9. In which religion is the concept of 'Karma' most centrally emphasized?

    Answer: Buddhism

    While originating in Hinduism, Karma is a fundamental concept in Buddhism, referring to the law of cause and effect of one's actions.

  10. The concept of 'Jugaad' in India refers to a specific cultural approach to problem-solving best described as:

    Answer: A frugal, innovative, and flexible workaround

    Jugaad is a Hindi word that means a 'hack' or a 'workaround.' It represents a flexible approach to problem-solving that uses limited resources in an innovative way, a highly valued trait in Indian culture.

  11. Which Buddhist tradition emphasizes meditation and mindfulness as the primary path to enlightenment?

    Answer: Zen Buddhism

    Zen Buddhism (a school of Mahayana) places strong emphasis on meditation (zazen) and direct experience as the path to awakening.

  12. Which of the following is a core concept in Hindu philosophy, referring to the cycle of birth, death, and rebirth?

    Answer: Samsara

    Samsara is the cycle of birth, death, and rebirth in Hindu, Buddhist, and Jain philosophies, driven by karma and one's actions.

  13. The concept of 'Ubuntu', often translated as 'I am because we are', originates from which region?

    Answer: Southern Africa

    Ubuntu is a Nguni Bantu term meaning 'humanity' that is often translated as 'I am because we are', emphasizing community and shared humanity.

  14. In Hinduism, what is the concept of 'Karma' most closely associated with?

    Answer: The cycle of cause and effect across lifetimes

    Karma is a fundamental concept in Hinduism (and other Indian religions) referring to the principle that every action has consequences that affect one's future, including across multiple lifetimes in the cycle of samsara.

  15. Which religious tradition involves the concept of 'Samsara' and the goal of 'Moksha'?

    Answer: Hinduism

    In Hinduism, Samsara is the cycle of death and rebirth, and Moksha is the liberation from this cycle, achieving ultimate spiritual freedom.
