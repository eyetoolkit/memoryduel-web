Answer key

All fifteen questions above, with the correct answer and the reasoning.

  1. Who is often called the 'Queen of Jazz'?

    Answer: Ella Fitzgerald

    Ella Fitzgerald is widely recognized as the 'Queen of Jazz' for her vocal prowess and influence.

  2. Which genre of music is characterized by improvised solos, swing rhythms, and blue notes?

    Answer: Jazz

    Jazz is defined by its swing rhythm, use of blue notes, call-and-response patterns, and emphasis on improvisation.

  3. Which composer is known for the 'Rhapsody in Blue'?

    Answer: George Gershwin

    Gershwin's 'Rhapsody in Blue' blends classical and jazz styles.

  4. What instrument is typically associated with jazz musician Louis Armstrong?

    Answer: Trumpet

    Louis Armstrong was a legendary jazz trumpeter and singer, famous for songs like 'What a Wonderful World'.

  5. Which jazz vocalist recorded the 1955 live album 'Ella Fitzgerald Sings the Cole Porter Song Book'?

    Answer: Ella Fitzgerald

    Ella Fitzgerald recorded the Cole Porter Song Book in 1955, a landmark vocal jazz album.

  6. Which instrument does jazz legend John Coltrane primarily play?

    Answer: Saxophone

    Coltrane was one of the most influential saxophonists (tenor and soprano) in jazz history.

  7. Which instrument is the lead instrument in a traditional jazz rhythm section?

    Answer: Drum kit

    The drum kit (often with brushes) sets the tempo and swing feel in a jazz rhythm section.

  8. Which musical instrument has 88 keys and is a staple in both classical and jazz music?

    Answer: Piano

    The piano is a versatile keyboard instrument capable of playing both melody and harmony, making it central to Western music.

  9. Which jazz trumpeter and vocalist recorded the 1959 album 'Kind of Blue' as a bandleader?

    Answer: Miles Davis

    Miles Davis led the historic 1959 recording 'Kind of Blue', a cornerstone of modal jazz.

  10. Which jazz musician is known for playing the trumpet and the albums 'Kind of Blue' and 'A Love Supreme'?

    Answer: Miles Davis

    Miles Davis was a pioneering jazz trumpeter whose albums 'Kind of Blue' (1959) and 'A Love Supreme' (1965, though 'A Love Supreme' is Coltrane's) - correction: 'Kind of Blue' is Miles Davis, 'A Love Supreme' is John Coltrane. Miles Davis also created 'Bitches Brew' and 'Sketches of Spain'.

  11. Which jazz pianist was a master of the 'stride' piano style, blending ragtime with jazz?

    Answer: Fats Waller

    Fats Waller (1904-1943) pioneered stride piano, a demanding style featuring a left-hand octaving technique that influenced generations of jazz pianists.

  12. Which jazz musician is known as 'Bird' and was a pioneer of bebop?

    Answer: Charlie Parker

    Charlie 'Bird' Parker was a revolutionary saxophonist who, along with Dizzy Gillespie, pioneered the bebop style of jazz in the 1940s.

  13. Which jazz legend, known as 'Satchmo', was a pioneering trumpet player and vocalist famous for 'What a Wonderful World'?

    Answer: Louis Armstrong

    Louis Armstrong (1901–1971), nicknamed 'Satchmo' (short for Satchelmouth), was one of the most influential figures in jazz history.

  14. Which jazz musician, known as 'The King of Swing' and famous for the song 'Sing, Sing, Sing', played the clarinet?

    Answer: Benny Goodman

    Benny Goodman (1909-1986), known as the 'King of Swing', was an American jazz clarinetist and bandleader whose recording of 'Sing, Sing, Sing' (1937) with his orchestra became an iconic swing era classic.

  15. Which jazz musician is known for pioneering the 'cool jazz' style and composing 'Take Five'?

    Answer: Dave Brubeck

    Dave Brubeck's quartet recorded 'Take Five' (from the album Time Out, 1959), one of the first jazz singles to go gold.
