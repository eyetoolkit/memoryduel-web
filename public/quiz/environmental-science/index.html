Answer key

All fifteen questions above, with the correct answer and the reasoning.

  1. What is the primary source of Earth's oxygen?

    Answer: Ocean phytoplankton

    Marine phytoplankton—microscopic ocean organisms—produce roughly half of Earth's oxygen through photosynthesis.

  2. Which of these is the primary gas absorbed by plants during photosynthesis?

    Answer: Carbon Dioxide

    Plants absorb carbon dioxide from the atmosphere and convert it into glucose and oxygen during photosynthesis.

  3. Which of these gases is most abundant in Earth's atmosphere?

    Answer: Nitrogen

    Nitrogen makes up about 78% of Earth's atmosphere, while oxygen accounts for roughly 21%.

  4. Which biome is characterized by very low temperatures, little precipitation, and no trees?

    Answer: Tundra

    Tundra is a cold, treeless biome with permafrost and limited rainfall.

  5. What is the primary purpose of the ozone layer?

    Answer: To absorb harmful ultraviolet radiation

    The ozone layer absorbs most of the Sun's harmful UV radiation, protecting life on Earth.

  6. What is the term for an organism that can produce its own food through photosynthesis?

    Answer: Autotroph

    Autotrophs, like plants and algae, produce their own food using sunlight and carbon dioxide.

  7. Which of these processes contributes to the water cycle?

    Answer: Condensation

    Condensation turns water vapor into liquid, forming clouds and precipitation.

  8. Which of these is a primary cause of ocean acidification?

    Answer: Excess carbon dioxide absorption

    The ocean absorbs about 30% of atmospheric CO2, which forms carbonic acid and lowers the water's pH.

  9. What is the term for the diversity of living organisms in a particular habitat or ecosystem?

    Answer: Biodiversity

    Biodiversity refers to the variety of life forms — species, genes, and ecosystems — in a given area.

  10. What is the primary greenhouse gas emitted by human activities, especially burning fossil fuels?

    Answer: Carbon Dioxide

    Carbon dioxide (CO₂) is the primary greenhouse gas produced by human activities, particularly the burning of coal, oil, and natural gas.

  11. What is the biological diversity of genes, species, and ecosystems called?

    Answer: Biodiversity

    Biodiversity refers to the variety of life in the world or in a particular habitat or ecosystem.

  12. What term describes the gradual increase in nitrogen and phosphorus compounds in a water body leading to algal blooms and oxygen depletion?

    Answer: Eutrophication

    Eutrophication occurs when excess nutrients stimulate algal growth; when the algae decompose, oxygen is consumed, creating dead zones.

  13. The ecological footprint metric estimates human demand on ecosystems. Which component contributes the largest share to the average ecological footprint in most developed nations?

    Answer: Carbon footprint (emissions from fossil fuel combustion)

    Carbon emissions dominate the footprint due to fossil fuel use.

  14. Which of the following is the largest coral reef system in the world?

    Answer: Great Barrier Reef

    The Great Barrier Reef in Australia is the world's largest coral reef system, stretching over 2,300 kilometers and comprising thousands of individual reefs.

  15. What phenomenon causes the seasons on Earth?

    Answer: The tilt of Earth's axis

    The seasons are caused by the 23.5-degree tilt of Earth's axis as it orbits the Sun, which changes the angle and duration of sunlight reaching different parts of the planet.
