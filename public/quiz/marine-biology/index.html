Answer key

All fifteen questions above, with the correct answer and the reasoning.

  1. Which marine mammal is known for using echolocation to find prey?

    Answer: Dolphin

    Dolphins emit sound waves and interpret returning echoes to locate food.

  2. What is the largest living structure on Earth, visible from space?

    Answer: The Great Barrier Reef

    The Great Barrier Reef is the world's largest coral reef system, composed of over 2,900 individual reefs and 900 islands, stretching over 2,300 kilometers off the coast of Australia.

  3. Which of these is the largest mammal in the world?

    Answer: Blue Whale

    The Blue Whale is the largest animal known to have ever lived, reaching lengths of up to 30 meters (98 feet) and weighing up to 200 tons.

  4. Which of the following is a mammal?

    Answer: Dolphin

    Dolphins are warm-blooded, breathe air, and nurse their young, making them marine mammals.

  5. What is the main cause of coral bleaching?

    Answer: Elevated sea temperatures

    When seawater temperature rises just 1–2 °C above normal summer maxima, corals expel their symbiotic zooxanthellae, turning white.

  6. Coral reefs are formed by the calcium carbonate skeletons of what organisms?

    Answer: Polyps

    Coral reefs are built by colonies of tiny animals found in marine waters called coral polyps. They secrete calcium carbonate to form a hard skeleton.

  7. Which human activity contributes most to introducing invasive species in aquatic ecosystems?

    Answer: Ballast water discharge

    Ships releasing ballast water can transport foreign organisms into new environments, facilitating invasions.

  8. Ocean acidification primarily results from increased atmospheric concentrations of which gas?

    Answer: Carbon dioxide

    CO2 dissolves in seawater forming carbonic acid, lowering pH and causing acidification.

  9. Outbreaks of the crown‑of‑thorns starfish (Acanthaster planci) on coral reefs are often linked to:

    Answer: Elevated nutrient runoff from land

    Nutrient enrichment boosts phytoplankton, providing food for starfish larvae and triggering outbreaks.

  10. Which process do coral reefs primarily rely on for their energy and growth?

    Answer: Symbiotic relationship with zooxanthellae algae

    Coral polyps have a mutualistic relationship with zooxanthellae, microscopic algae that live in their tissues. The algae photosynthesize and provide up to 90% of the coral's energy needs.

  11. What primary factor drives the seasonal phytoplankton blooms in temperate oceans?

    Answer: Increased light and nutrient availability

    Spring increases in sunlight and upwelling nutrients trigger phytoplankton blooms.

  12. In the carbon cycle, what is the main role of ocean phytoplankton?

    Answer: Fix carbon dioxide through photosynthesis

    Phytoplankton are primary producers that fix CO2 into organic carbon via photosynthesis, forming the base of marine food webs.

  13. Which factor most strongly influences the vertical distribution of planktonic foraminifera in the open ocean?

    Answer: B. Light availability (photosynthetic symbionts)

    Foraminifera that host algal symbionts concentrate in the photic zone where light drives symbiont photosynthesis.

  14. Which of the following is an example of a keystone species in a marine ecosystem?

    Answer: Sea otter

    Sea otters control sea urchin populations, preserving kelp forest health, making them keystone predators.

  15. Which phenomenon describes the formation of low‑oxygen or oxygen‑free zones in marine environments due to excessive nutrient inputs?

    Answer: Eutrophication‑induced hypoxia

    Eutrophication‑induced hypoxia results from nutrient runoff fueling algal growth, whose decomposition depletes dissolved oxygen, creating dead zones.
