Answer key

All fifteen questions above, with the correct answer and the reasoning.

  1. Which major festival is known as the 'Festival of Lights'?

    Answer: Diwali

    Diwali is a major Hindu festival celebrated with lights and lamps to symbolize the victory of light over darkness.

  2. Which animal is considered sacred in Hinduism and often associated with Lord Ganesha?

    Answer: Elephant

    The elephant, particularly the head of Ganesha, is sacred in Hinduism, representing wisdom and the removal of obstacles.

  3. Which festival is known as the Festival of Lights?

    Answer: Diwali

    Diwali, celebrated primarily by Hindus, Sikhs, and Jains, is known as the Festival of Lights and symbolizes the victory of light over darkness.

  4. What is the significance of the 'Kumkum' or 'Bindi' worn by many Hindu women?

    Answer: A symbol of marital status and spiritual energy

    The bindi/kumkum symbolizes the third eye and is traditionally worn by married women as a sign of auspiciousness.

  5. What is the name of the Hindu spring festival of colors, celebrating the triumph of good over evil and the arrival of spring?

    Answer: Holi

    Holi, the 'Festival of Colors,' celebrates the divine love of Radha and Krishna and the victory of good (represented by Prahlada) over evil (Holika).

  6. The festival of Holi, known as the 'Festival of Colors', is celebrated in which South Asian country?

    Answer: India

    While celebrated across South Asia (including Nepal and Pakistan), Holi is most prominently and widely associated with India, where it is a major public holiday.

  7. Which religion practices the concept of 'Samsara', the cycle of rebirth?

    Answer: Hinduism

    Samsara is the cycle of death and rebirth, a core concept in Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, and Sikhism.

  8. In Hindu tradition, what does the tilak (mark on the forehead) typically symbolize?

    Answer: Third eye or spiritual insight

    The tilak represents the 'third eye' or seat of spiritual wisdom and is applied during religious ceremonies and daily worship.

  9. Which ancient Hindu text describes the four stages of life (ashramas)?

    Answer: Manusmriti

    The Manusmriti outlines the four ashramas: student, householder, retiree, and ascetic.

  10. Which festival involves people throwing colored powder at each other to celebrate the arrival of spring?

    Answer: Holi

    Holi, known as the 'Festival of Colors', symbolizes the triumph of good over evil and the arrival of spring, celebrated by playfully throwing colored powders and water.

  11. In which religion is the concept of 'Dharma' considered a central moral and ethical principle?

    Answer: Hinduism

    Dharma is a key concept in Hinduism referring to cosmic law, duty, and the right way of living.

  12. In Hinduism, what is the concept of cyclical cosmic creation, preservation, and destruction known as?

    Answer: Trimurti

    The Trimurti represents the cosmic functions of creation (Brahma), preservation (Vishnu), and destruction (Shiva) in Hindu theology.

  13. Which Hindu month‑long period, occurring during the monsoon season in the lunar month of Shravan, is dedicated to Lord Shiva and includes fasting on Mondays (Shravan Somvar)?

    Answer: Shravan

    Shravan is a sacred month for Shiva devotees, marked by fasting, pilgrimages, and special Monday prayers.

  14. In the Hindu festival of Navaratri, which deity is primarily worshipped across nine nights?

    Answer: Durga

    Navaratri honors the Goddess Durga and her various forms, celebrating the victory of the Goddess over the buffalo demon Mahishasura.

  15. In Hinduism, what is the name of the festival that celebrates the goddess Durga's victory over the buffalo demon Mahishasura?

    Answer: Durga Puja

    Durga Puja celebrates the goddess Durga's victory over the buffalo demon Mahishasura, symbolizing the triumph of good over evil. It is primarily celebrated in West Bengal, Bangladesh, and among Bengali communities worldwide. Navaratri is a nine-night festival honoring the goddess Durga but has different regional focuses.
