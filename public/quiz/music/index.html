Music Quiz

Most music quizzes pick a decade and ask who sang what. This one splits the difference: half the questions are craft — how many strings, how many lines, what a word on the score actually instructs you to do — and half are records and artists.

The craft half is where people are surprised. Almost everyone has seen a stave and few can say how many lines it has; almost everyone knows the word crescendo and fewer can say precisely what changes. Twenty questions, answered with the reason rather than just the term.

Answer key

All 20 questions, with the correct answer and the reasoning.

  1. How many lines does a standard musical staff have?

    Answer: Five

    Five lines and four spaces. Ledger lines are added above or below when a note falls outside that range.

  2. How many keys does a standard piano have?

    Answer: 88

    Eighty-eight keys, 52 white and 36 black, spanning a little over seven octaves.

  3. Which band recorded Bohemian Rhapsody?

    Answer: Queen

    Queen released it in 1975. It runs nearly six minutes and was unusual for having no chorus at all.

  4. How many strings does a standard acoustic guitar have?

    Answer: Six

    Six. A twelve-string guitar doubles each of those strings in pairs, which is where its shimmer comes from.

  5. Which singer is known as the King of Pop?

    Answer: Michael Jackson

    Michael Jackson. Elvis is the King of Rock and Roll; James Brown is the Godfather of Soul.

  6. Which member of The Beatles played bass?

    Answer: Paul McCartney

    Paul McCartney played bass, though he started as a guitarist. Lennon played rhythm guitar and Starr played drums.

  7. What does the Italian marking forte tell a performer to do?

    Answer: Play loudly

    Forte means strong or loud. Piano means soft, which is why the instrument is named the soft-loud, or pianoforte.

  8. Which instrument family does the trumpet belong to?

    Answer: Brass

    Brass. The trumpet is played by buzzing the lips into a mouthpiece, not by vibrating a reed, which is what separates brass from woodwind.

  9. Who composed The Four Seasons?

    Answer: Antonio Vivaldi

    Vivaldi wrote the four violin concertos around 1723. Each season comes with a sonnet, possibly written by Vivaldi himself.

  10. What is a group of four musicians called?

    Answer: Quartet

    A quartet. The string quartet — two violins, a viola and a cello — is the standard form, and most classical composers wrote several.

  11. In which city did jazz originate?

    Answer: New Orleans

    New Orleans, at the start of the twentieth century. Chicago and New York became the centres later, as musicians moved north.

  12. What does BPM measure in music?

    Answer: Tempo

    Beats per minute, which is tempo. A typical pop song sits between 90 and 120 BPM.

  13. Who composed the opera The Magic Flute?

    Answer: Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart

    Mozart, in 1791, the year he died. It premiered in Vienna and was an immediate popular success.

  14. Which is the highest female voice type?

    Answer: Soprano

    Soprano. Mezzo-soprano and contralto sit below it, and alto usually refers to a choral part rather than a solo voice.

  15. Which country did reggae come from?

    Answer: Jamaica

    Jamaica, in the late 1960s. It grew out of ska and rocksteady, and Bob Marley took it worldwide in the 1970s.

  16. What does crescendo mean?

    Answer: Gradually louder

    Gradually louder. A sudden loud accent is a sforzando, and gradually faster is accelerando.

  17. Which album did The Beatles release in 1969 with a photo of the band on a zebra crossing?

    Answer: Abbey Road

    Abbey Road. The cover was shot outside the EMI studios in north London and has been imitated ever since.

  18. What does singing a cappella mean?

    Answer: Without instruments

    Without instrumental accompaniment. The term comes from the Italian for 'in the manner of the chapel'.

  19. How many notes are in a chromatic scale across one octave?

    Answer: Twelve

    Twelve, one for every semitone. A major scale uses seven of those twelve, which is why it sounds like a scale rather than a slide.

  20. Flamenco music comes from which country?

    Answer: Spain

    Spain, and specifically Andalusia. It combines Romani, Moorish and Andalusian traditions, with guitar, song and dance as one form.

What this quiz covers

The theory questions are written so that a wrong answer tells you something. If you pick the wrong number of staff lines you will remember the right one, because the explanation says what the lines are for rather than just counting them.

Nothing here depends on a chart position or a sales figure that changes, with one exception — the King of Pop label, which is a nickname rather than a statistic.

  • Notation and instrument basics: the five-line staff, the 88-key piano, six guitar strings and the chromatic scale.
  • Italian terms that are really instructions: forte, crescendo, and what each one asks a player to change.
  • Voice and ensemble: the highest female voice type, and what a group of four players is called.
  • Artists and records: Queen, The Beatles, Michael Jackson, and the bassist everyone can name but misidentifies.
  • Origins: where jazz was born, where reggae came from, and which country flamenco belongs to.

Frequently asked questions

How many lines does a musical staff have?
Five. Notes sit on the lines and in the four spaces between them, and ledger lines extend the staff when a note falls outside its range.
How many keys does a standard piano have?
88 — 52 white and 36 black. That range covers just over seven octaves, and it is the reason the piano can play almost any written part.
Who is known as the King of Pop?
Michael Jackson. The nickname was popularised in the 1980s, partly by Elizabeth Taylor introducing him at an awards ceremony.
Where did jazz originate?
New Orleans, around the start of the twentieth century. It grew out of brass band music, blues and ragtime, and the city's mix of cultures is why it sounds the way it does.
What does a cappella mean?
Singing without instrumental accompaniment. The phrase is Italian for 'in the manner of the chapel', where voices were used without instruments.
