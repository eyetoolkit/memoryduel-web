Food Quiz

Food trivia splits neatly in two: the questions about what a dish is made of, which you either know or do not, and the questions about where it came from, which are usually more surprising than people expect.

This quiz has 22 questions across both — ingredients, national dishes, festivals built around eating, and a few production facts that get quoted wrong more often than right.

Every answer is explained, the full key sits under the quiz, and there is no sign-up. The questions are about food in general rather than any one cuisine, so expect the spread to jump from Mexico to Japan to Ethiopia.

Answer key

All 22 questions, with the correct answer and the reasoning.

  1. What fruit is famously called the "king of fruits"?

    Answer: Durian

    Durian is widely known as the "king of fruits" due to its large size and strong odor.

  2. Which fruit contains the highest amount of vitamin C per 100 g?

    Answer: Guava

    Guava provides roughly 228 mg of vitamin C per 100 g, more than oranges or other common fruits.

  3. Which cheese is traditionally used on a pizza Margherita?

    Answer: Mozzarella

    Mozzarella, ideally the fresh buffalo version, along with tomato and basil — the three colours of the Italian flag.

  4. Which grain is the most produced worldwide?

    Answer: Maize

    Maize (corn) is the most produced grain globally, with annual output exceeding 1,000 million tonnes.

  5. Which country consumes the most chocolate per capita?

    Answer: Switzerland

    Switzerland has the highest per‑capita chocolate consumption, averaging about 12 kilograms per person each year.

  6. Which country is credited with inventing pizza?

    Answer: Italy

    Modern pizza originated in Naples, Italy, during the 18th century.

  7. Which country is the world's largest producer of coffee?

    Answer: Brazil

    Brazil produces roughly one-third of the world's coffee, the top producer.

  8. What is the main ingredient in traditional hummus?

    Answer: Chickpeas

    Traditional hummus is made primarily from blended chickpeas, blended with tahini, lemon juice, and garlic.

  9. Which country has the highest per‑capita coffee consumption in the world?

    Answer: Finland

    According to the International Coffee Organization, Finland tops the list with about 12 kg per person per year.

  10. What is the main ingredient in guacamole?

    Answer: Avocado

    Guacamole is made primarily from mashed avocados, seasoned with salt, lime juice, and often onion, tomato, and cilantro.

  11. What is the main ingredient in traditional Japanese miso soup?

    Answer: Fermented soybean paste

    Miso soup is made from dashi (soup stock) and miso, which is a fermented paste made from soybeans, salt, and koji.

  12. During the Jewish festival of Hanukkah, which of the following is traditionally fried in oil to commemorate the miracle of the temple?

    Answer: Latkes

    Latkes (potato pancakes) are a traditional food for Hanukkah, fried in oil to celebrate the miracle of the oil lasting eight nights in the rededicated Second Temple.

  13. The 'Dragon Boat Festival' (Duanwu Festival), celebrated with boat races and eating zongzi, originated in which country?

    Answer: China

    The Dragon Boat Festival originated in China over 2,000 years ago, commemorating the death of the poet Qu Yuan. It involves dragon boat races and eating sticky rice dumplings called zongzi.

  14. In which country did the tradition of drinking tea originate thousands of years ago?

    Answer: China

    Tea originated in ancient China, where it was first used as a medicinal drink before becoming a daily beverage and an integral part of Chinese culture.

  15. What is the main ingredient in traditional Japanese sushi?

    Answer: Rice

    Sushi is defined by its use of vinegared rice, which is the primary ingredient, often combined with seafood and vegetables.

  16. During which festival do people traditionally eat mooncakes?

    Answer: Mid-Autumn Festival

    Mooncakes are a traditional Chinese bakery product eaten during the Mid-Autumn Festival, symbolizing family reunion and togetherness.

  17. In many Western countries, what is traditionally hidden in a Christmas pudding for good luck?

    Answer: A coin

    It is customary to hide a coin in the pudding; finding it is said to bring good fortune.

  18. What traditional Mexican dish is associated with the Day of the Dead?

    Answer: Pan de Muerto

    Pan de Muerto is a sweet bread traditionally baked and eaten during Day of the Dead celebrations.

  19. What is the main herb in traditional Italian pesto?

    Answer: Basil

    Pesto alla genovese is built on basil, pine nuts, garlic, Parmesan and olive oil, pounded together rather than cooked.

  20. Which spice is the most expensive in the world by weight?

    Answer: Saffron

    Saffron is harvested by hand from crocus flowers, and roughly 150,000 flowers are needed for one kilogram of the dried spice.

  21. Tofu is made from which ingredient?

    Answer: Soybeans

    Tofu is curdled soy milk, pressed into blocks. The process is close to cheesemaking, which is why the texture varies with how hard it is pressed.

  22. Marzipan is made primarily from which nut?

    Answer: Almond

    Marzipan is ground almonds and sugar. The ratio decides whether it is used for shaping or for eating.

What this quiz covers

Most food quizzes drift towards either a single cuisine or a list of brand names. This one deliberately stays on things that are stable — a dish's ingredients and the country it is associated with do not change, whereas which chain sells the most burgers does.

Where a fact is commonly repeated incorrectly, the explanation says so. The most-produced grain and the most-eaten staple are different answers, for instance, and the quiz asks for the distinction rather than the headline.

  • Ingredients: what guacamole, hummus, miso soup and pesto are actually made from.
  • Origins: where pizza, tea, the Dragon Boat Festival and the King Cake come from.
  • Festival food: mooncakes, latkes, pan de muerto and the coin hidden in a Christmas pudding.
  • Production and consumption: the most produced grain, the largest coffee producer, the country that eats the most chocolate per person.
  • The expensive end: the spice that costs more by weight than most metals.

Frequently asked questions

What is the most consumed food in the world?
Rice is the most widely consumed staple food by number of people who depend on it daily. Maize is the most produced grain by weight, but a large share of it goes to animal feed and fuel rather than directly to plates.
Which spice is the most expensive in the world?
Saffron, by a wide margin. It is hand-harvested from crocus flowers and it takes roughly 150,000 flowers to produce a single kilogram.
Is a tomato a fruit or a vegetable?
Botanically a fruit, because it develops from the flower and contains seeds. In cooking and in US tariff law it has been treated as a vegetable, which is where the confusion comes from.
Which country invented pizza?
Italy, and specifically Naples, where the modern flatbread with tomato was sold to working people in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries. The Margherita version is named after a queen of Italy.
Why does this quiz mix cuisines?
Because the questions are about food as a subject rather than about one tradition. A quiz that only asks about Italian food tests whether you have eaten in Italy, not whether you know how food works.
