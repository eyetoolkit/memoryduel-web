Sports Quiz

Sports knowledge is oddly uneven. People who watch every weekend can be certain that a cricket side has eleven players and entirely unsure how many points a touchdown is worth, because they only ever see the number after it has been added up.

This quiz goes after exactly that layer — the rules and measurements that sit underneath the games. Team sizes, scoring values, distances, and the venues where a particular sport is essentially defined. Twenty questions, each explained, so a wrong answer tells you why the number is what it is.

Answer key

All 20 questions, with the correct answer and the reasoning.

  1. How many players from one team are on the pitch in a football match?

    Answer: Eleven

    Eleven including the goalkeeper. A side is not allowed to continue with fewer than seven players on the pitch.

  2. How often are the Summer Olympic Games held?

    Answer: Every four years

    Every four years, a period called an Olympiad. The Winter Games were moved onto an offset cycle in 1994 so the two events alternate.

  3. How many players from one team are on court in basketball?

    Answer: Five

    Five. A team must have five to start a game, but if players foul out it can finish with fewer rather than forfeit.

  4. In tennis, what is a score of zero called?

    Answer: Love

    Love, probably from the French l'oeuf for egg. A scoreless game is called love-all rather than zero-zero.

  5. How many holes are played in a standard round of golf?

    Answer: Eighteen

    Eighteen. A nine-hole course is half a round, which is why it is called the front or back nine.

  6. Which sport is played with a shuttlecock?

    Answer: Badminton

    Badminton. The shuttlecock is designed to decelerate quickly, which is why the game rewards touch as much as power.

  7. How long is an Olympic swimming pool?

    Answer: 50 metres

    Fifty metres for long course. Short course pools are 25 metres, and world records are kept separately for each.

  8. How many players are in a cricket team on the field?

    Answer: Eleven

    Eleven per side. A twelfth man can field as a substitute but is not allowed to bat or bowl in most formats.

  9. Which country won the first FIFA World Cup, held in 1930?

    Answer: Uruguay

    Uruguay, who hosted it and beat Argentina in the final. They won again in 1950, also at home.

  10. What is the maximum break in a standard frame of snooker?

    Answer: 147

    147 — fifteen reds each followed by a black, then the six colours. A free ball awarded before the first red can technically raise the maximum to 155.

  11. How many points is a touchdown worth in American football?

    Answer: Six

    Six points, with the extra point attempt afterwards making seven the usual total. A field goal is worth three.

  12. Which sport is played at Wimbledon?

    Answer: Tennis

    Tennis, on grass — the only Grand Slam still played on the surface. The tournament has run since 1877.

  13. How many rings are on the Olympic flag?

    Answer: Five

    Five, one for each inhabited continent as they were counted in 1913: Africa, Asia, Europe, the Americas and Oceania.

  14. How many strikes make an out in baseball?

    Answer: Three

    Three strikes. A foul ball with two strikes does not count as the third unless it is caught.

  15. What is the official distance of a marathon?

    Answer: 42.195 kilometres

    42.195 kilometres, or 26 miles 385 yards, fixed after the 1908 London Olympics and standardised in 1921.

  16. Which gymnastics apparatus is named after a horse?

    Answer: Pommel horse

    The pommel horse, named for the handles that resemble a saddle. It is used by men only in elite competition.

  17. In which city were the first modern Olympic Games held in 1896?

    Answer: Athens

    Athens. Paris hosted the second Games in 1900, which were held alongside the World's Fair and ran for months.

  18. How many players from one team are on court in volleyball?

    Answer: Six

    Six. Beach volleyball uses two per side, which is why the two versions feel like different sports.

  19. Which sport is traditionally called 'the sport of kings'?

    Answer: Horse racing

    Horse racing, because of the cost of breeding and keeping thoroughbreds. Polo also claims the phrase, which is where the confusion comes from.

  20. How many rounds are in a modern championship boxing match?

    Answer: Twelve

    Twelve for men's championship bouts. Fifteen was standard until the 1980s, when the rules were changed after a series of deaths in the ring.

What this quiz covers

Every answer here is a rule, not a result, which is deliberate: rule questions do not go stale, and they cannot be argued with. No 'who won last year', no records that a single season can break.

Where two sports share a number — football and cricket both field eleven — the questions are written to make the difference visible, because the reason a cricket side has eleven is not the same as the reason a football side does.

  • Team sizes across sports that do not agree: eleven in football and cricket, five in basketball, six on a volleyball court.
  • Scoring values: six points for a touchdown, three strikes for an out, and the maximum break in snooker.
  • Distances and sizes: an Olympic pool, a marathon, and a round of golf.
  • Venues and events: Wimbledon, the first modern Olympics in 1896, and the first World Cup in 1930.
  • The Olympic layer — how often the Games are held, and what the five rings actually stand for.

Frequently asked questions

How many players are on a football team on the pitch?
Eleven, including the goalkeeper. A match cannot continue if either side drops below seven players.
How often are the Summer Olympics held?
Every four years. The gap is called an Olympiad, and the Winter Games were moved onto the same four-year cycle in 1994 so the two no longer fall in the same year.
How long is a marathon?
42.195 kilometres, or 26 miles 385 yards. The odd distance was fixed at the 1908 London Games and then standardised.
Why is zero called love in tennis?
Usually explained as coming from the French 'l'oeuf', meaning egg, for the shape of a zero, though the etymology is not settled. The alternative story, that it comes from playing for love, has no evidence behind it.
What is the maximum break in snooker?
147, achieved by potting all fifteen reds with blacks and then clearing the colours. A free ball at the start can technically push the maximum to 155.
