Answer key

All fifteen questions above, with the correct answer and the reasoning.

  1. The Magna Carta, signed in 1215, limited the power of which English king?

    Answer: King John

    King John of England was forced by his barons to sign the Magna Carta, which established the principle that everyone, including the king, was subject to the law.

  2. The Magna Carta, a foundational document in the history of democracy, was signed in which country?

    Answer: England

    The Magna Carta was agreed to by King John of England at Runnymede in 1215.

  3. Which empire was the largest contiguous land empire in world history?

    Answer: Mongol Empire

    At its height in the 13th–14th centuries, the Mongol Empire under Genghis Khan and his successors became the largest contiguous land empire ever, spanning from Eastern Europe to East Asia.

  4. Who is credited with starting the Protestant Reformation in 1517?

    Answer: Martin Luther

    Martin Luther, a German monk, posted his Ninety-five Theses in 1517, challenging Catholic Church practices and sparking the Reformation.

  5. The Renaissance, a period of great cultural and artistic achievement, originated in which country during the 14th century?

    Answer: Italy

    The Renaissance began in Italy, particularly in city-states like Florence. It marked a transition from the Middle Ages to modernity, emphasizing humanism and reviving classical learning.

  6. The Black Death pandemic in Europe peaked during which century?

    Answer: 14th century

    The Black Death (bubonic plague) ravaged Europe primarily during the mid-14th century, killing roughly one-third of the population.

  7. Which African kingdom, known for its wealth and the legendary ruler Mansa Musa, flourished from the 13th to 16th centuries?

    Answer: Mali Empire

    The Mali Empire in West Africa grew extremely wealthy through control of gold and salt trade routes, especially under Mansa Musa in the 14th century.

  8. Which empire was ruled by Genghis Khan and his successors in the 13th century?

    Answer: The Mongol Empire

    Genghis Khan founded the Mongol Empire in 1206, which became the largest contiguous land empire in history.

  9. The Battle of Hastings in 1066 led to which ruler conquering England?

    Answer: William the Conqueror

    William, Duke of Normandy, defeated King Harold II at Hastings, becoming William the Conqueror.

  10. The Battle of Yarmouk in 636 AD was a decisive conflict between the Byzantine Empire and which rising power?

    Answer: The Rashidun Caliphate

    The Battle of Yarmouk in 636 AD was a major defeat for the Byzantine Empire and a decisive victory for the Rashidun Caliphate, leading to the Muslim conquest of the Levant.

  11. The Hundred Years' War was fought between England and which other country?

    Answer: France

    The Hundred Years' War (1337–1453) was fought between England and France over territorial disputes and the English claim to the French throne.

  12. The Battle of Hastings took place in which year?

    Answer: 1066

    The Battle of Hastings in 1066 was a decisive Norman victory that led to the Norman conquest of England and the end of Anglo-Saxon rule.

  13. The Thirty Years' War, which devastated much of Europe, began in which year?

    Answer: 1618

    The Thirty Years' War began in 1618 in the Holy Roman Empire and lasted until 1648, reshaping the political map of Europe.

  14. The ancient city of Constantinople fell to the Ottoman Turks in which year?

    Answer: 1453

    Constantinople fell to Sultan Mehmed II's Ottoman forces on May 29, 1453, ending the Byzantine Empire.

  15. The Thirty Years' War, one of the most destructive conflicts in European history, was primarily a religious war between which two religious groups?

    Answer: Protestants and Catholics

    The Thirty Years' War (1618-1648) was initially a religious conflict between Protestant and Catholic states in the Holy Roman Empire, though it eventually became a broader political struggle.
