Answer key

All fifteen questions above, with the correct answer and the reasoning.

  1. What does the acronym 'FPS' stand for in video games?

    Answer: Frames Per Second

    FPS measures how many images are rendered per second, but is also commonly used for 'First Person Shooter' games.

  2. In which sport is the term 'grand slam' also used to describe winning all four major tournaments in a single season?

    Answer: Tennis

    In tennis, a Grand Slam means winning the Australian Open, French Open, Wimbledon, and US Open in the same calendar year.

  3. What does 'solo' mean in music?

    Answer: Played by one person

    'Solo' refers to a piece or section performed by a single musician.

  4. What does the acronym 'Wi-Fi' commonly stand for in everyday use, despite having no official meaning?

    Answer: Wireless Fidelity

    Though the Wi-Fi Alliance states it has no specific meaning, 'Wireless Fidelity' is the popular backronym often associated with the technology.

  5. In which sport is the term 'birdie' used to describe a score of one stroke under par on a hole?

    Answer: Golf

    In golf, a 'birdie' means finishing a hole in one stroke less than par, while an 'eagle' is two under and a 'bogey' is one over.

  6. Which musical term means to play a note louder than surrounding notes?

    Answer: Forte

    Forte (f) is the dynamic marking indicating that a note or passage should be played loudly. Piano (p) means the opposite.

  7. In cricket, what is the term for scoring exactly six runs by hitting the ball over the boundary without it bouncing?

    Answer: Six

    Hitting the ball directly over the boundary rope without bouncing scores six runs, called a 'six'. Scoring four runs (bouncing before crossing) is called a 'four'.

  8. In which sport are 'aces' and 'volleys' common terms?

    Answer: Tennis

    An ace is a serve that wins a point directly, and a volley is hitting the ball before it bounces.

  9. In the video game 'Fortnite', what is the term used when a player completely eliminates an opponent?

    Answer: Elimination

    While 'Victory Royale' is winning the match, a single kill is referred to as an elimination.

  10. In swimming, what does 'IM' stand for in the event '200m IM'?

    Answer: Individual Medley

    IM stands for Individual Medley, a swimming event where athletes must use all four competitive strokes (butterfly, backstroke, breaststroke, freestyle) in a specific order.

  11. What is the term for a situation in competitive gaming where a player or team has an insurmountable lead?

    Answer: Snowball

    A 'snowball' refers to a compounding advantage where an early lead grows larger over time, making a comeback nearly impossible.

  12. Which of these musical terms indicates a sudden, strong accent?

    Answer: Sforzando

    'Sforzando' (or 'sfz') indicates a sudden, strong accent. 'Legato' means smooth, 'Staccato' means short/detached, and 'Leggiero' means light and graceful.

  13. In the Tour de France, what is the nickname given to the leader of the general classification (wearing the yellow jersey)?

    Answer: Le Maillot Jaune

    The leader of the general classification wears the 'maillot jaune' (yellow jersey).

  14. In competitive 'Counter-Strike: Global Offensive' (CS:GO), what is the term for a round where a team wins by eliminating all opponents?

    Answer: Ace

    In CS:GO, an 'Ace' refers to a single player eliminating all 5 members of the enemy team in a round, while a 'Clutch' refers to a player winning a round when they are the last one alive on their team. These are distinct terms.

  15. In cricket, what is the term used when a bowler takes three wickets in three consecutive deliveries?

    Answer: Hat-trick

    Taking three wickets in three consecutive balls is known as a hat-trick. A 'golden duck' is being out on the first ball faced.
