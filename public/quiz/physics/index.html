Answer key

All fifteen questions above, with the correct answer and the reasoning.

  1. What is the boiling point of water at sea level in degrees Celsius?

    Answer: 100°C

    Water boils at 100°C (212°F) at standard atmospheric pressure at sea level.

  2. What is the unit of force in the SI system?

    Answer: Newton

    The newton (N) is the SI unit of force, defined as the force needed to accelerate 1 kg by 1 m/s².

  3. At what temperature does water boil at standard sea-level pressure?

    Answer: 100°C

    Water boils at 100°C (212°F) at standard atmospheric pressure (1 atm).

  4. Which of these states of matter has a fixed volume but takes the shape of its container?

    Answer: Liquid

    Liquids maintain a constant volume but adapt to the shape of their container.

  5. What is the force that attracts objects toward the center of the Earth?

    Answer: Gravity

    Gravity is the universal force of attraction between masses; on Earth it pulls objects downward.

  6. What type of energy is stored in food?

    Answer: Chemical energy

    Food contains chemical potential energy that the body releases through metabolism.

  7. What is the process called when water changes from liquid to gas?

    Answer: Evaporation

    Evaporation occurs when water molecules gain enough energy to escape into the atmosphere as vapor.

  8. Who developed the theory of general relativity?

    Answer: Albert Einstein

    Albert Einstein published his general theory of relativity in 1915, describing gravity as the curvature of spacetime.

  9. A particle moving in a circular path at constant speed experiences which type of acceleration?

    Answer: Centripetal acceleration

    Centripetal acceleration points toward the center of the circle, keeping the particle in circular motion despite constant speed.

  10. According to Newton's second law, if the net force acting on an object doubles while its mass remains constant, how does its acceleration change?

    Answer: It also doubles

    Newton’s second law states a = F/m, so doubling the force doubles the acceleration.

  11. Which quantum mechanical principle states that it is impossible to simultaneously know both the exact position and exact momentum of a particle?

    Answer: Heisenberg uncertainty principle

    Heisenberg's uncertainty principle sets a fundamental limit on how precisely position and momentum can be known at the same time.

  12. Which of these particles is exchanged to produce the strong nuclear force?

    Answer: Gluon

    Gluons are the force carriers of the strong nuclear force, which binds quarks together inside protons and neutrons.

  13. Which subatomic particle was experimentally discovered in 2012, confirming the mechanism that gives particles mass?

    Answer: Higgs boson

    The Higgs boson was discovered at CERN's Large Hadron Collider in 2012. It is the quantum excitation of the Higgs field, which imparts mass to fundamental particles via the Brout-Englert-Higgs mechanism.

  14. What is the term for the phenomenon where light bends as it passes from one medium to another?

    Answer: Refraction

    Refraction is the change in direction of a wave passing from one medium to another or from a gradual change in the medium. Reflection is bouncing off, diffraction is bending around obstacles, and dispersion is separating into colors.

  15. What principle states that no two identical fermions can occupy the same quantum state simultaneously?

    Answer: Pauli Exclusion Principle

    The Pauli Exclusion Principle explains electron shell structure and the stability of matter.
