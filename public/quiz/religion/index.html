Answer key

All fifteen questions above, with the correct answer and the reasoning.

  1. Which festival marks the beginning of the Christian liturgical year and celebrates the birth of Jesus?

    Answer: Christmas

    Christmas celebrates the birth of Jesus Christ on December 25th in most Christian traditions.

  2. Which major world religion was founded by Siddhartha Gautama, known as the Buddha?

    Answer: Buddhism

    Buddhism originated in India around the 5th century BCE, based on the teachings of Siddhartha Gautama.

  3. What is the name of the Hindu festival celebrating the arrival of spring, characterized by throwing colored powders?

    Answer: Holi

    Holi, often called the 'Festival of Colors', celebrates the eternal and divine love of Radha and Krishna and the triumph of good over evil.

  4. Which religious festival marks the birth of Jesus Christ and is celebrated worldwide on December 25?

    Answer: Christmas

    Christmas is the Christian celebration of the birth of Jesus Christ, observed on December 25 by most Christian denominations.

  5. In which religion is the concept of Karma central to understanding the cycle of rebirth?

    Answer: Buddhism

    Karma is a key concept in Dharmic religions like Buddhism and Hinduism, referring to the law of cause and effect.

  6. The 'Holi' festival, known as the 'Festival of Colors', is celebrated primarily in which country and religion?

    Answer: India (Hinduism)

    Holi is a Hindu spring festival celebrated in India and Nepal, famous for participants throwing colored powder at each other.

  7. In the Christian liturgical calendar, what is the name of the 40-day period of fasting and prayer leading up to Easter?

    Answer: Lent

    Lent is a 40-day period (excluding Sundays) of fasting, prayer, and penance in preparation for Easter, observed by many Christian denominations.

  8. Which festival is known as the 'Festival of Lights' in India and symbolizes the victory of light over darkness?

    Answer: Diwali

    Diwali is a major Hindu festival celebrated with oil lamps (diyas) and fireworks to represent the triumph of good over evil and light over darkness.

  9. The Protestant Reformation is generally considered to have begun with Martin Luther's posting of his Ninety-five Theses in which year?

    Answer: 1517

    Martin Luther posted his Ninety-five Theses on the door of the Castle Church in Wittenberg in 1517, challenging the Catholic Church's practices and sparking the Reformation.

  10. Which religious festival commemorates the liberation of the Israelites from Egyptian slavery?

    Answer: Passover

    Passover (Pesach) celebrates the Exodus from Egypt, observed with the Seder meal and removal of leavened bread.

  11. In Hinduism, what is the name of the festival that celebrates the birth of the deity Krishna, often involving dramatic reenactments?

    Answer: Janmashtami

    Janmashtami celebrates the birth of Lord Krishna, one of the most revered deities in Hinduism, with devotees fasting, singing, and performing dances.

  12. Which religious tradition celebrates 'Yom Kippur', the Day of Atonement, with fasting and prayer?

    Answer: Judaism

    Yom Kippur is the holiest day in Judaism, observed with a 25-hour fast and intensive prayer as Jews seek forgiveness for sins.

  13. What is the name of the traditional Jewish festival commemorating the exodus from Egypt, during which unleavened bread is eaten?

    Answer: Passover (Pesach)

    Passover (Pesach) commemorates the Israelites' liberation from slavery in Egypt.

  14. Which ancient philosophy, originating in China, emphasizes harmony with the natural order and is often associated with the yin-yang symbol?

    Answer: Taoism

    Taoism (Daoism) centers on the Tao (the Way) and promotes living in harmony with the fundamental nature of the universe, represented by the yin-yang.

  15. In which religion is the 'Five Pillars' doctrine a central framework?

    Answer: Islam

    The Five Pillars of Islam—declaration of faith, prayer, charity, fasting during Ramadan, and pilgrimage to Mecca—are fundamental practices.
