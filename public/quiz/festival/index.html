Answer key

All fifteen questions above, with the correct answer and the reasoning.

  1. The Day of the Dead (Día de los Muertos) is a vibrant festival honoring deceased loved ones. In which country is it most famously celebrated?

    Answer: Mexico

    Día de los Muertos is a Mexican holiday where families welcome back the souls of their deceased relatives for a brief reunion that includes food, drink, and celebration.

  2. Which religion is celebrated during the festival of Diwali, often known as the Festival of Lights?

    Answer: Hinduism

    Diwali is a major festival in Hinduism, symbolizing the victory of light over darkness and good over evil, though it is also celebrated by Jains and Sikhs.

  3. Which Japanese children's festival, observed on November 15, celebrates the healthy growth of girls aged three and seven and boys aged three and five?

    Answer: Shichi-Go-San

    Shichi-Go-San is a traditional Japanese festival honoring the growth of children.

  4. Which country celebrates the festival of "Songkran" with massive water fights to mark the traditional Thai New Year?

    Answer: Thailand

    Songkran is the Thai New Year festival famous for water fights, celebrated primarily in Thailand.

  5. In the Hindu festival of Holi, what is thrown between participants?

    Answer: Colored powders and water

    Holi, known as the Festival of Colors, involves throwing colored powders (gulal) and water at each other, symbolizing the arrival of spring and the triumph of good over evil.

  6. What is the name of the Japanese New Year celebration?

    Answer: Shōgatsu

    Shōgatsu is the traditional Japanese New Year, celebrating the arrival of spring and renewal.

  7. What is the central tradition of the Japanese 'Obon' festival?

    Answer: Honoring ancestral spirits

    Obon is a Buddhist-Confucian custom to honor the spirits of one's ancestors, believed to return to the world of the living during this time.

  8. In Judaism, what is the name of the eight-day festival commemorating the rededication of the Second Temple in Jerusalem?

    Answer: Hanukkah

    Hanukkah, also called the Festival of Lights, lasts eight nights and is celebrated by lighting a menorah, eating fried foods, and giving gifts.

  9. What is the significance of the Chinese 'Red Envelope' (hongbao) tradition?

    Answer: It is given during Lunar New Year to bring good luck

    Red envelopes containing money are given during Lunar New Year and other celebrations to symbolize good luck and ward off evil.

  10. Which African festival celebrates the Yoruba deity Oshun with a procession to a river?

    Answer: Osun-Osogbo

    Osun-Osogbo is a two-week festival in Nigeria honoring the Yoruba goddess of fertility and water, Oshun.

  11. Which ancient Persian festival, celebrated on the vernal equinox, marks the beginning of the new year and the first day of spring?

    Answer: Nowruz

    Nowruz, meaning 'New Day,' is the Iranian New Year celebrated on the spring equinox, symbolizing rebirth and the renewal of nature, and is observed by various cultures across Central and South Asia.

  12. The 'Fiesta de la Candelaria' in Mexico, where the Virgin of Charity is paraded, usually takes place in which month?

    Answer: February

    Celebrated on February 2nd, it blends Catholic beliefs with indigenous traditions, often featuring traditional dance.

  13. Which festival marks the Buddhist celebration of the Buddha's birth, enlightenment, and death?

    Answer: Vesak

    Vesak (Buddha Day) commemorates the birth, enlightenment, and passing of the Buddha.

  14. The 'Inti Raymi' festival, a major celebration of the Inca sun god, is still observed today primarily in which country?

    Answer: Peru

    Inti Raymi is an ancient Inca festival honoring the sun god Inti, now celebrated as a cultural reenactment in Cusco, Peru.

  15. In the Philippines, what is the traditional festival where devotees carry replicas of the Sto. Niño (Child Jesus) through the streets?

    Answer: Sinulog

    The Sinulog Festival in Cebu, Philippines, honors the Sto. Niño (Child Jesus) with street dancing, colorful costumes, and the carrying of Santo Niño replicas through the streets.
